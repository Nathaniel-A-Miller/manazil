رقيع بن عبيد بن صيني، من الوافر

Ruqayʿ ibn ʿUbayd ibn Ṣayfī said:

قال رقيع بن عبيد بن صيفي:

Have you not stopped at the timeworn campsite traces,the tribe’s dwellings in the years gone by?
ألم تلمم على الدمن البواليديار الحي في الحجج الخوالي
Every rain-laden cloud has effaced them, and the passingof days and nights since you left.
عفتها كل معصرة، ومرمن الأيام بعدك والليالي
It left only a few of their recognizable traces,speechless when questioned.
فأبقى من معارفها قليلاعيبا حين يسأل بالسؤال
There they dwelt; and every pleasure of lifein this world must pass away.
بها عمروا، وكل نعيم عيشمن الدنيا يصير إلى زوال
They were the defenders, and among them weremen of bounty and far-reaching power.
هم كانوا الحماة وكان فيهمذوو الأفضال والأيدي الطوال
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 3: The printed عيبا does not yield a clear sense here. The translation tentatively reads عييا, meaning unable to speak or answer, describing the surviving traces.
  • Line 5: الأيدي الطوال, literally “long hands,” may signify great generosity rather than the extensive power adopted here.

البحتري، من الكامل

And al-Buḥturī said:

وقال البحتري:

They have grown familiar with parting, as though it were their homeland,which they approach only by going far away.
ألفوا الفراق كأنه وطن لهملا يقربون إليه حتى يبعدوا
Each day, a campsite left by their tribegrows desolate, and a dwelling of theirs turns to wilderness.
في كل يوم دمنة من حيهمتقوى، وربع منهم يتأبد
Steady the breasts of the sturdy she-camels for a haltamong the standing remnants, as though they were Musnad writing.
أسند صدور اليعملات بوقفةفي الماثلات كأنهن المسند
Ruins forced to proclaim their decayby the raging winds, coming and coming again,
دمن تقاضاهن إعلان البلىهوج الرياح الباديات العود
until they vanished. How can what stands endure,while time ranges round its edges?
حتى فنين، وما البقاء لواقفوالدهر في أطرافه يتردد
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 3: The unvocalized أسند can be read as an imperative, 'steady,' or as a first-person present, 'I steady.' The imperative is adopted here. المسند is understood as Musnad, the ancient South Arabian script to which the remnants are compared.

البحتري، من الكامل

He also said:

وقال أيضا:

Between al-Saqīfa, al-Liwā, and al-Ajraʿlie traces of dwellings, left to the four winds.
بين السقيفة فاللوى فالأجرعدمن حبسن على الرياح الأربع
It is as though their landmarks held within themwhat the tormented lover's breast contains.
فكأنما ضمنت معالمها الذيضمنته أحشاء المحب الموجع
If spring's rains would obey me,spring would quench the thirst of those dwellings.
ولو أن أنواء الربيع تطيعنيلشفى الربيع غليل تلك الأربع
How lovely the days are, except thatthey pass us by, and once gone, never return.
ما أحسن الأيام إلا أنهاتمضي بنا، وإذا مضت لم ترجع
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 1: Al-Saqīfa, al-Liwā, and al-Ajraʿ are treated as place names; their precise locations are uncertain. The latter two can also denote terrain: curving sands and a sandy tract.

البحتري، من المنسرح

He also said:

وقال أيضا:

The valley of Khabt, though its caravans have gone far away,will not leave us without its ruins stirring our longing.
ما جو خبت وإن نأت ظعنهتاركنا أو تشوقنا دمنه
Whenever a new dwelling is taken, one grows attached to itthrough familiarity, until it seems one's homeland.
إذا استجدت دارا تعلقهابالإلف حتى كأنها وطنه
By God, it never ceases to bewilder us—this age's joy or its sorrow.
تالله ما إن يني يدلهناسرور هذا الزمان أو حزنه
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: I take جو خبت as 'the valley of Khabt'; the precise location is uncertain. The construction أو تشوقنا is unusual here; I understand it as 'without its ruins stirring our longing,' though 'until its ruins stir our longing' is also possible.
  • Line 2: The subjects and pronouns are unclear. Unvocalized استجدت permits several readings, including 'I/you took a new dwelling,' whereas تعلقها and وطنه appear to refer to an unidentified third-person subject ('he grows attached to it'; 'his homeland'). The impersonal translation is provisional; the text may be faulty.

أبو تمام، من الكامل

And Abū Tammām said:

وقال أبو تمام:

Ruins—he visited them and said, “Peace!”How often a visit has undone the knot of his patience!
دمن ألم بها فقال سلامكم حل عقدة صبره الإلمام
I faced the caravan’s mounts until they coveredmy feet with dust; they treated me harshly and blamed me.
نحرت ركاب الركب حتى يغبروارجلى، لقد عنفوا علي ولاموا
They persisted in reproaching me until they made it seemthat stopping at the dwellings was forbidden.
وقفوا على اللوم حتى خيلواأن الوقوف على الديار حرام
May not a single day pass withouta rain cloud in its breast for your two dwelling places.
لا مر يوم واحد إلا وفيأحشائه لمحلتيك غمام
I once beheld you; shall I behold you in your early splendor,with life still fresh and time a youth?
ولقد أراك فهل أراك بغرةوالعيش غض والزمان غلام
Years of union whose long span made one forgetthe thought of parting—as though they were mere days.
أعوام وصل كان ينسى طولهاذكر النوى فكأنها أيام
Then days of estrangement came upon us, followedby searing grief—as though they were years.
ثم انبرت أيام هجر أردفتبجوى أسى فكأنها أعوام
Then those years and their people passed away;both they and those years were as dreams.
ثم انقضت تلك السنون وأهلهافكأنهم وكأنها أحلام
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 2: The wording نحرت ركاب الركب حتى يغبروا رجلى is obscure and may be corrupted. The translation tentatively takes نحرت as “faced” and يغبروا رجلى as “covered my feet with dust,” but the connection between these actions remains unclear.
  • Line 5: The sense of بغرة is uncertain: it may refer to early splendor or freshness, as translated, or to an unguarded moment.
  • Line 6: The unvocalized ينسى is taken as يُنسي, “makes one forget,” with طولها (“their long span”) as its subject.

مهيار، من الكامل

And Mihyār said:

وقال مهيار:

Ruins, like the track of trailing reins,their tight strands unravelled.
دمن كمسحبة الأزمة مسحلا إمرارها
Their realities have died,but their false semblance and borrowed guise endure.
ماتت حقائها وخلد زورها ومعارها
Long has grown the night of the sand-driving windsacross their open ground—and their day.
وامتد ليل السافيات بجوها، ونهارها
For them, if they grow barren, I havea streaming eye from which they may draw sustenance.
عندي لها إن أجدبتوكافة تمتارها
They have grown accustomed to streaming tears,as though the ruins were the tears’ own lashes.
أنست بإسبال الدموع، كأنها أشفارها
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: The wording كمسحبة الأزمة مسحلا إمرارها is difficult and may be corrupt. The translation provisionally takes مسحبة الأزمة as the track of dragged reins, and مسحلا إمرارها as tightly twisted strands being loosened or unravelled.
  • Line 2: حقائها appears corrupt. The translation conjectures حقائقها, ‘their realities,’ in contrast with زورها ومعارها, ‘their false semblance and borrowed guise.’
  • Line 5: The pronouns in كأنها أشفارها are ambiguous. The translation takes the ruins as resembling eyelashes over which the speaker’s tears flow.

زهير بن أبي سلمى، من الطويل

Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā, whose name was Rabīʿa ibn Razāḥ al-Muzanī, said:

قال زهير بن أبي سلمى، واسمه ربيعة بن رزاح المزني:

Is this a silent remnant of Umm Awfā’s camp,at Ḥawmānat al-Darrāj and al-Mutathallam?
أمن أم أوفى دمنة لم تكلمبحومانة الدراج فالمتثلم
Her dwellings at al-Raqmatayn are likeretraced tattoo marks upon the sinews of a wrist.
ديار لها بالرقمتين كأنهامراجع وشم في نواشر معصم

[Al-nawāshir]: the sinews of the forearm.

[النواشر] : عصب الذراع

Wide-eyed wild cattle and white gazelles walk there, one after another,and their young rise from every resting-place.
بها العين والآرام يمشين خلفةوأطلاؤها ينهضن من كل مجثم
I stood there after twenty years;only with difficulty, after pondering, did I recognize the dwelling.
وقفت بها من بعد عشرين حجةفلايا عرفت الدار بعد توهم
When I recognized the dwelling, I said to its deserted ground:“Good morning to you, dwelling-place, and may you remain unharmed!”
فلما عرفت الدار قلت لربعها:ألا انعم صباحا أيها الربع واسلم

النابغة الذبياني، من البسيط

And al-Nābigha al-Dhubyānī said:

وقال النابغة الذبياني:

Turn aside and greet the traces of Nuʿm’s dwelling;what do you greet but a ditch and stones?
عوجوا فحيوا لنعم دمنة الدارماذا تحيون من نؤى وأحجار
It lies deserted, empty of Nuʿm, and has been alteredby raging winds bearing billowing, swirling dust.
أقوى وأقفر من نعم وغيرهاهوج الرياح بهابي الترب موار
I stopped there at midday, asking the dwellingabout Nuʿm’s folk, on a trusty she-camel hardened by journeys.
وقفت فيها سراة اليوم أسألهاعن آل نعم أمونا عبر أسفار
But Nuʿm’s dwelling remained mute; it did not speak to us,though, had it spoken to us, it had much to tell.
فاستعجمت دار نعم ما تكلمناوالدار لو كلمتنا ذات أخبار
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 3: «أمونًا عبر أسفار» appears to describe a dependable she-camel, strong or seasoned in travel. The exact construction and the sense of «عبر أسفار» are uncertain; «أمونًا» may be the delayed object of «وقفت», meaning that the speaker halted his camel.

كثير بن عبد الرحمن، من الطويل

And Kuthayyir ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Khuzāʿī said:

وقال كثير بن عبد الرحمن الخزاعي:

Has the site of a ruined camp stirred you, and dwellingsleft empty, effaced by the scouring rain clouds?
أهاجك مغنى دمنة ومساكنخلت وعفاها المعصرات السوافن

[Al-sawāfin]: he means al-sawāfī, those that sweep away.

(السوافن) : أراد السوافي

The dwellings of al-Saʿdī’s daughter, when the knot of her bondwas firm, and her kindness was yours to find.
ديار ابنة السعدي إذ عقد حبلهامتين، وإذ معروفها لك عاهن

[ʿĀhin]: present, at hand.

(عاهن) : حاضر

Ever since my mustache first sprouted, on Laylā’s accountI have kept quelling rancor and making peace, down to this day.
وما زلت في ليلى لدن طرشاربيإلى اليوم أطفئ إحنة وأداجن
On Laylā’s account I bear a grudge against some people,and on Laylā’s account grudges are borne against me.
وأحمل في ليلى لقوم ضغينةوتحمل في ليلى على الضغائن
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: المعصرات السوافن is taken as rain clouds that scour away the traces. المعصرات can also denote rain-bringing winds, and السوافن suggests scraping or sweeping away.
  • Line 4: على appears to be a typing or OCR error for عليّ, ‘against me.’ The translation also reads تحمل as passive تُحمل, ‘are borne.’

And Dhū al-Rumma said:

وقال ذو الرمة:

You yearn for Mayy as one yearns for home,called by longing, then forced back by his fetters.
تحن إلى مي كما حن نازعدعاه الهوى فارتد في قيده قسرا
I said: “Halt, my two companions, at the campsite’s tracesat Dhū al-Ramth, whose dwellings have long lain deserted.”
فقلت: أربعا يا صاحبي بدمنةبذي الرمث قد أقوت منازلها عصرا
Your eyes have shed tears there, until it is as thoughby pouring out tears there, they were fulfilling a vow.
أرشت بها عيناك حتى كأنمايحلان من سفح الدموع بها نذرا
There is no Mayy—only a visit at Mushrifor al-Zurq to the desolate traces of her encampments.
ولا مي إلا أن تزور بمشرفأو الزرق من أطلالها دمنا قفرا
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: نازع denotes one longing for home; the fetters may suggest a tethered camel, but the referent is not explicit.
  • Line 2: Dhū al-Ramth is a place name; its location is unidentified.
  • Line 4: Mushrif and al-Zurq appear to be place names; their locations and vocalizations are uncertain. The elliptical ولا مي إلا أن تزور is understood as denying Mayy’s presence and leaving only a visit to her deserted campsites.

He also said:

وقال أيضا:

Was it a campsite’s traces between al-Qilāt and Shāriʿthat made you yearn like a youth, until your eye kept shedding tears?
أمن دمنة بين القلات وشارعتصابيت حتى ظلت العين تدمع

أجل عبرة ظلت إذا ما وزعتهابحلمي أبت منها عوارض تسرع
Longing does not bring back the time that has passed,nor is a dwelling’s ruin a place for a man to despair.
وما يرجع الوجد الزمان الذي مضىوما للفتى في دمنة الدار مجزع
That evening I had no recourse, save that I wasabsorbed in picking up pebbles and tracing lines at the dwelling.
عشية مالي حيلة غير أننيبلقط الحصى والخط في الدار مولع
I draw a line and erase it, then draw it againwith my palm, while crows perch at the dwelling.
أخط وأمحو الخط ثم أعيدهبكفي والغربان في الدار وقع
It is as though a Persian spearhead had struck mein the liver—no, love’s burning anguish hurts still more.
كأن سنانا فارسيا أصابنيعلى كبدي، بل لوعة الحب أوجع
Ah, if only the days of al-Qilāt and Shāriʿwould return to us, and then all life come to an end!
ألا ليت أيام القلات وشارعرجعن لنا، ثم انقضى العيش أجمع

  • Line 2: I take عوارض to mean recurrent or sudden surges of tears; the compressed phrasing أبت منها عوارض تسرع leaves the precise construction uncertain.

وقال أيضا:

Is it because of an abandoned campsite in the hollow, Jaww Jalājil,that your companion streams with tears and is distraught?
أمن دمنة بالجو جو جلاجلزميلك منهل الدموع جزوع؟

Will those days of ours return, O Mayy—those at Dhū al-Rimth—or not? Is there no returning for them?

Even had the departing people not stirred my longing, it would have been stirredby gray doves settled among the dwellings.

They answered one another, making everyone who had loved weep—mourning birds for whom no tears flowed.
تجاوبن فاستبكين من كان ذا هوىنوائح لم تذرف لهن دموع

  • Line 18: The supplied hemistich division appears misplaced: the likely division is after قلب فتى rather than after جمعت. It has been preserved as supplied.   • Line 20: ونفسي على باقية is problematic; على may represent عليّ, but the construction remains uncertain. The translation gives the probable contrast between being dead and still having one’s soul. وضعت is read as “I was brought low.” The identity of الحافظات, “the guardians,” is unclear.
  • Line 22: غببت appears to be a typing or OCR error for غُيِّبت, “I was hidden away,” which is followed in the translation.
  • Line 23: إذ ال أمر appears to have faulty spacing or lettering. The translation reads إذ الأمر, “since the situation,” though إذ آل أمر, “since matters have come to,” may also have been intended.

عروة بن حزام، من الطويل

And ʿUrwa ibn Ḥizām said:

وقال عروة بن حزام:

O you two ravens on the dwelling’s traces between us,are you whispering together of parting from ʿAfrāʾ?
ألا يا غرابي دمنة الدار بينناأبا لهجر من عفراء تنتجيان؟
If what you say is true, then fly awaywith my flesh to your two nests, and eat me.
فإن كان حقا ما تقولان فانهضابحلمي إلى وكريكما فكلاني
For ʿAfrāʾ, above all others, has my purest affection,yet ʿAfrāʾ turns away from me and holds back.
فعفراء أصفى الناس عندي مودةوعفراء عني المعرض المتواني
By God, I have not told your secret to a friend,however faithful, nor have my lips uttered it—
فوالله ما حدثت سرك صاحبانصيحا، ولا فاهت به الشفتان
save that I once said to my companionone forenoon, as our two she-camels bore us swiftly along:
سوى أنني قد قلت يوما لصاحبيضحى، وقلوصانا بنا تخدان
“Ah, for love of ʿAfrāʾ, how dear is the confluenceof Naʿām and Birk, where the two meet!”
ألا حبذا من حب عفراء ملتقىنعام، وبرك، حيث يلتقيان
Translator's notes (4)
  • Line 1: أبا لهجر appears to be a spacing error for أبالهجر, ‘is it of parting …?’
  • Line 2: بحلمي (‘with my forbearance’ or ‘with my understanding’) appears corrupted. The translation assumes بلحمي, ‘with my flesh,’ which fits the ravens’ eating him.
  • Line 3: The first hemistich is ambiguous: it may instead describe ʿAfrāʾ as the sincerest of people in her affection, in the speaker’s estimation.
  • Line 6: Naʿām and Birk appear to be two valleys. The vocalization of برك, rendered here as Birk, is uncertain.

أبو وجزة، من الطويل

And Abū Wajza al-Saʿdī said:

وقال أبو وجزة السعدي:

Whose camp-trace is this at al-Naʿf, its ground effaced,its remnants altered, its freshness worn away?
لمن دمنة بالنعف عاف صعيدهاتغير باقيها ومح جديدها
Saʿda’s, from the year of al-Hurayma, when between usthere was affection, and her turning away had not yet distressed us.
لسعدة في عام الهريمة إذ بناتصاف، وإذ لما يرعنا صدودها
And when she herself was skilledin dalliance, yet kept herself from passion.
وإذ هي أما نفسها فأريبةللهو، وأما عن صبي فتذودها
She snares men’s hearts with her coquetry,but her nature is untamed—we cannot snare her.
تصيد ألباب الرجال بدلهاوشيمتها وحشية لا نصيدها
Like a towering cloud of the autumn rains as it lets down its showers,lightning flashing within it, its neck gleaming white.
كباسقة الوسمي ساعة أسبلتتلألأ فيها البرق وابيض جيدها
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: Al-Naʿf may be a place name or a descriptive term for sloping ground beside a mountain or valley.
  • Line 2: I could not identify the event or period called ‘the year of al-Hurayma’; the vocalization of الهريمة is uncertain.
  • Line 3: The reading صبي is unclear. ‘Passion’ provisionally takes it as a form of ṣibā, youthful infatuation or folly, possibly with a final-letter error. The pronoun in فتذودها is taken to refer back to نفسها: she restrains herself.

مهيار، من الكامل

And Mihyār said:

وقال مهيار:

They departed, taking my tender daysin their wake, and my life of ease.
رحلوا بأيامي الرقاق علىآثارهم، وبعيشي السهل
I lingered after them beside an ailing remnant,which knew decay and wasted as things waste.
وعكفت بعدهم على ضمنعرف البلى فبلى كما يبلى
My body and the ruined dwelling, both wasted away,lament to each other the scattering of our company.
جسدي، ودمنته بما نحلايتشاكيان تصدع الشمل
A dwelling where yesterday, out of passionate love, we laidits windblown dust upon our eyes like kohl.
مغنى وضعنا أمس من شعفسافي ثراه مواضع الكحل
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: ضمن is uncertain. I tentatively read it as ضَمِن, ‘ailing,’ describing the abandoned dwelling figuratively; it may instead be a textual error.
  • Line 2: The syntax of فبلى كما يبلى is unclear. The following جسدي (‘my body’) may supply the subject of يبلى, giving ‘wasted as my body wastes,’ but it also appears to begin the paired subject ‘my body and the ruined dwelling’ in line 3.
