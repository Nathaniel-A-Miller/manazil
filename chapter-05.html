الأحوص، من الرمل

Al-Ahwas said:

قال الأحوص:

By my life, I spent my nightlike one afflicted with painful illness.
قد لعمري بت ليلىكأخي الداء الوجيع
Grief, my intimate companion,lay nearer to me than my bedmate.
ونجي الهم منيبات أدنى من ضجيعي
Whenever I saw an encampmentlying empty, my tears overflowed.
كلما أبصرت ربعاخاليا فاضت دموعي
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: Read ليلى as ليلي, 'my night'; the final letter appears to be a spelling or digitization error, rather than the name Laylā.
  • Line 2: The construction نجي الهم is ambiguous: literally 'grief’s confidant,' it is understood here as grief personified as an intimate companion.

أبو تمام، من الكامل

And Abu Tammam said:

وقال أبو تمام:

O once-fresh abode of theirs, I see you worn away,and your guests’ fare is anguish and lingering pain.
أقشيب ربعهم أراك دريساوقرى ضيوفك لوعة ورسيسا
Though you are held captive to decay, nowmy tears are held captive to you until death.
ولئن حبست على البلى لقد اغتدىدمعي عليك إلى الممات حبيسا
I see your dwelling places desolate, althoughyou were once a familiar abode, full of company.
وأرى ربوعك موحشات بعد ماقد كنت مألوف المحل أنيسا
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 1: رسيس can mean a lingering trace or an abiding feeling, especially of love or pain. Its precise sense here is uncertain; it is rendered as “lingering pain.”

أبو تمام، من البسيط

وقال أيضا:

Greet Salmā’s dwelling at Dhū Salam;it bears the mark of passing days and age.
سلم على الربع من سلمى بذي سلمعليه وسم من الأيام والقدم

من الخفيف

It is said: One day Yahya ibn Khalid ibn Barmak left his house to go to al-Rashid. As he passed through one of the courtyards of his palace, he saw written on one of its walls:

قيل: خرج يحيى بن خالد بن برمك يوما من داره يريد الرشيد، فمر ببعض أفنية قصره، فرأى على بعض حيطانه مكتوبا:

Enjoy your ease, house of Barmak,and watch for it—when will it come?
أنعموا آل برمكوارقبوها متى هيه
And watch for Time to turnupon you with a calamity.
وارقبوا الدهر أن يدور عليكم بداهيه

  • Line 1: The wording «وارقبوها متى هيه» is elliptical. The feminine pronoun apparently anticipates the calamity named in line 2; «هيه» is read as «هي» with a pause-ending h.

أبو نواس، من الطويل

He was struck silent and distressed by this. Then, that same day, Abū Nuwās came in to him and recited:

فوجم وجزع لذلك، ثم دخل - في ذلك اليوم - عليه أبو نواس، فأنشده:

O dwelling worn by decay, abasement is plainupon you; I have not betrayed you in my love.
أربع البلى إن الخشوع لبادعليك وإني لم أخنك ودادي
Forgive me, then, that you should liecaptive to winds and the downpour of morning clouds,
فمعذرة مني إليك بأن ترىرهينة أرواح وصوب غواد
while I have no means to ward harm off from you;for here I am not addressing Suʿād.
ولا أدرأ الضراء عنك بحيلةفما أنا فيها قائل لسعاد
Though once you exchanged my misery for comfort,you have now given my eye grit instead of sleep.
وإن كنت قد بدلت بؤسي بنعمةلقد بدلت عيني قذى برقاد

Until he reached the words:

إلى أن بلغ إلى قوله:

Farewell to the world when you are gone,sons of Barmak, going forth at evening or at dawn.
سلام على الدنيا إذا ما فقدتمبني برمك من رائحين وغاد
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 3: The reference to Suʿād is uncertain, possibly contrasting this lament with conventional love poetry. The second hemistich could also be read as a question: 'What, then, am I to say to Suʿād about it?'
  • Line 4: I take the verbs as addressing the dwelling: it formerly relieved the poet's misery but now causes sleepless grief. The unvocalized كنت and بدلت also permit first-person readings; the direction of exchange in the second hemistich is not entirely certain.

الفند الزماني، من الرمل

He took it as an ill omen, and that very night misfortune befell him. Muhammad ibn Wasiʿ—may God have mercy on him—used to pass by the dwellings of his brethren after their deaths and call out to them: “O so-and-so! O so-and-so!” Then he would turn to himself and say: “They have died, by God! A sandal that has lost its mate will surely soon follow its companion.” Al-Find al-Zimmani, whose name was Shahl ibn Shayban ibn Rabiʿa ibn Zimman, said:

فتطير، فنكب في تلك الليلة. كان محمد بن واسع - رحمه الله - يمر برباع إخوانه بعد موتهم، فيناديهم: أي فلان، أي فلان، ثم يرجع إلى نفسه فيقول: ماتوا والله، وإن نعلا فقدت أختها لسريعة اللحاق بصاحبتها. وقال الفند الزماني، واسمه شهل بن شيبان بن ربيعة بن زمان:

Has the deserted campsite saddened you, and the dwellings?A man's weeping for a campsite is a loss.
أشجاك الربع أقوى والدياروبكاء المرء للربع خسار
What sense has a man faced with his fate,who seeks refuge in grief when a dwelling saddens him?
أي لب لامرئ في قدرهعائذ بالحزن إذ تشجيه دار
He weeps only for those who dwelt there,then withdrew far from him, once their abode lay distant.
إنما يبكي الألى كانوا بهافانتأوه بعد ما شط المزار
Time destroys and builds tirelessly,and Time's destruction of a dwelling is its rebuilding.
يخرب الدهر ويبني جاهداوخراب الدهر للدار عمار

(This is an inversion; he means that its being built up is its ruin):

(هذا قلب، أراد عمارتها خراب لها) :

You who weep for what has passed you by,desist, for some words bring shame.
أيها الباكي على ما فاتهأقصرن عنك، فبعض القول عار
The people's anguish avails nothing whencalamity befalls them, save a change.
ليس يغني جزع القوم إذاوقع الأمر بهم إلا الغيار

(He is saying: it does them no good to grieve, but rather to bring about change.)

(يقول: ليس يغني عنهم أن يجزعوا، ولكن أن يغيروا) .

Grieve over what has happened, or do not grieve:the roof has fallen in and the wall has collapsed.
فاجزعوا للأمر، أو لا تجزعواقد تداعى السقف وأنهار الجدار
Translator's notes (5)
  • Line 2: في قدره is ambiguous. I have provisionally understood قدر as 'fate'; it could instead concern the man's standing or worth. Its grammatical connection to the rest of the line is uncertain.
  • Line 3: فانتأوه is an unusual form with an attached object pronoun. I have provisionally understood it as 'withdrew far from him,' referring to the grieving man, but the construction and the pronoun's referent are uncertain.
  • Line 4: The second hemistich literally identifies Time's destruction of a dwelling with its flourishing or rebuilding. The intended sense of this apparent reversal is unclear; the translation preserves it.
  • Line 6: الغيار can denote change, but its precise meaning here, and the logic of 'save a change' after 'avails nothing,' are unclear. The text may be defective.
  • Line 7: أنهار appears to be a spelling or transcription error for انهار, 'collapsed,' which is the reading translated.

And ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa al-Makhzūmī said:

وقال عمر بن أبي ربيعة المخزومي:

Question the dwelling in its decay, you two, and say:“You have stirred a long yearning in us this morning.
سائلا الربع بالبلى وقولاهجت شوقا لنا الغداة طويلا

أين حي حلوك إذ أنت محفوف بهم آهلا أراك جميلا
It said: “They all set out and departed,against my will—if only I had had some way to prevent it!”
قال: ساروا بأجمع فاستقلواوبكرهي لو استطعت سبيلا
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: بالبلى is uncertain: rendered here as “in its decay,” but it may conceal a place-name or a textual error.
  • Line 3: لو استطعت سبيلا (“if I could find a way”) is elliptical; “to prevent it” supplies the understood wish to prevent their departure.

And Abū Ḥayya al-Numayrī said:

وقال أبو حية النميري:

Stop, you two, at what you recognize of my dwellings,even if my tears have outrun my utmost fortitude.
قفا عند مما تعرفان ربوعيوإن سبقت فرط العزاء دموعي

نحيي على طول البلى رسم دمنةكأن لم تكن من آلفين جميع
And what do we greet of traces that seem,at the foot of Salmānayn, like a worn and tattered cloth?
وماذا نحيي من رسوم كأنهابأسفل سلمانين سحق صديع
As though three doves at the campsitehad alighted, never wearying of their long stay.
كأن حمامات ثلاثا بربعهاوقعن فما يسأمن طول وقوع
I am indeed lovesick, as you know; yet Iresist some of my soul’s desires.
وإني لصب ما علمت وإننيلبعض هوى نفسي لغير مطيع
Translator's notes (4)
  • Line 1: The wording عند مما تعرفان ربوعي is syntactically irregular and may contain a copying or OCR error. The translation provisionally takes it to mean ‘at what you recognize of my dwellings.’
  • Line 3: Salmānayn appears to be a place-name; its precise location is uncertain.
  • Line 4: The three motionless ‘doves’ may represent the three hearthstones left at the campsite, though the stones are not explicitly named.
  • Line 5: The unvocalized ما علمت is taken as ‘as you know’; it could also be read as ‘as far as I know.’
