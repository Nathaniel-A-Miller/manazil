On weeping for family and brothers

فصل في بكاء الأهل والإخوان
Index 882, page 410
أنشدها علي بن أبي طالب، من الطويل

I say: This chapter belonged at the beginning of the book, since abodes and dwellings are wept over only for the family, brethren, and loved ones who inhabited them. But I deferred it so as to close the book with it. It is related of the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib—may God be pleased with him—that he would often quote these verses:

قلت: هذا الفصل كان موضعه صدر الكتاب؛ إذ كانت المنازل والديار إنما تبكي لسكانها من الأهل والإخوان والأحباب، لكني أخرته؛ لأختم به الكتاب. روى عن أمير المؤمنين علي بن أبي طالب - رضوان الله عليه - أنه كان كثيرا ما يتمثل بهذا الشعر:

Indeed, by God, I see that I am not of you,nor you of me, though you are my kin.
ألا قد أرى والله أن لست منكمولا أنتم مني، وإن كنتم أهلي
I am a sojourner whose departure is at hand;whoever greets him greets him as he stands ready to leave.
وإني ثوى قد أحم انطلاقهيحييه من حياه وهو على رجل
I am leaving you without companions,following my brothers who went before me.
ومنطلق منكم بغير صحابةوتابع إخواني الذين مضوا قبلي
Did I not keep company with ʿAmr and Mālik,and Adham, who went forth among horsemen or men on foot?
ألم أك قد صاحبت عمرا ومالكاوأدهم يغدو في فوارس أو رجل؟!
I kept company with Shaybān and with Ḍābiʾ,and the proud, tall Banū Shibl were my companions.
وصاحبت شيبانا وصاحبت ضابئاوصاحبني الشم الطوال بنو شبل
Those were my brothers; they have gone their way.Remembering them nearly makes me lose my reason.
أولئك إخواني مضوا لسبيلهميكاد ينسيني تذكرهم عقلي
People untouched by grief say, “Put them out of mind”;but one like me will never forget men like them.
يقول أناس أخلياء: تناسهموليس بناس مثلهم أبدا مثلي
Those are my dear friends; whenever I remember them,I weep from an eye whose tears are my kohl.
أولاك أخلائي إذا ما ذكرتهمبكيت بعين ماء عبرتها كحلي
And when the cold winds blew,and night’s blackness drew one household close to another,
وكانوا إذا ما القر هبت رياحهوضم سواد الليل رحلا إلى رحل
they would make the two jugular veins and the sciatic vein flow with the sword,when no people’s herdsman went out for milk.
يدرون بالسيف الوريدين والنساإذا لم يقم راعي أناس إلى رسل
When they met their peers in battle, they killed them;and if they themselves faced death, they did not shudder at being killed.
إذا ما لقوا أقرانهم قتلوهموإن قتلوا لم يقشعروا من القتل
How many a captive’s bonds you have undone,and bucket after bucket of blood you have spilled!
فكم من أسير قد فككتم قيودهوسجل دم أهرقتموه على سجل
Translator's notes (5)
  • Line 2: I read ثوى as ثَوِيّ, ‘guest’ or ‘sojourner.’ The expression على رجل appears to describe someone poised to depart, though its precise sense here is uncertain.
  • Line 4: ʿAmr, Mālik, and Adham cannot be securely identified from this passage. رجل is read as a collective meaning ‘men on foot,’ contrasted with horsemen.
  • Line 5: Shaybān, Ḍābiʾ, and the Banū Shibl cannot be securely identified from this passage.
  • Line 9: رحل can mean a saddle or, by extension, a dwelling or household. I take the image to be households drawing close together at night.
  • Line 10: The bleeding vessels apparently belong to livestock slaughtered to feed others when milk was scarce, but the animals are not explicitly named. النسا is the vessel traditionally called the ‘sciatic vein.’

يزيد بن ضبة بن مقسم، من المنسرح

And Yazīd ibn Ḍabba ibn Miqsam said:

وقال يزيد بن ضبة بن مقسم:

Your clinging heart has not forgotten Salmā;how can you yield to youthful passion when you are seasoned?
لم ينس سلمى فؤادك السدكوكيف تصبو وأنت محتنك؟
Had your love been for one alone, you would havedesisted; but your love is divided.
لو كان ما واحدا هواك لقدأقصرت، لكن هواك مشترك
Salmā says—and she found it strange—
تقول سلمىواستنكرت
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: The contrast between واحداً and مشترك is ambiguous: it may mean love directed toward one person versus several, as translated, or one-sided versus mutual love. The particle ما appears to be pleonastic.
  • Line 3: The line is incomplete. استنكرت can mean finding something strange or expressing disapproval; its object is missing. Without vowels or further context, its subject is also uncertain; the translation takes it as Salmā.
Poem

: How strange!

: عجبا

Why are parts of you being ravaged?
ما بال أشياء منك تنتهك
I said: From grief and sorrow—the sons of ʿAwf and Mālik have perished.
فقلت: من ترحة، ومن أسفأبناء عوف ومالك هلكوا
They left wide breaches around me, which gaped open;those they left behind could not close them.
خلوا فجاجا علي فانخرقتلم يستطع سدهن من تركوا
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 1: The expression «أشياء منك تنتهك», literally “things of/from you are violated,” is unclear. “Parts of you being ravaged” is tentative; the reference might instead be to things belonging to the addressee being violated.

وعيل العبسي، من الطويل

And Wuʿayl al-ʿAbsī said:

وقال وعيل العبسي:

Have you not seen me after those who departed one after another—those with whose support I gave and stood my ground?
ألم ترني بعد الذين تتابعواوكانوا الألى أعطي بهم وأمانع
I am like one with cracks that nearly shatter his bone;blows will not spare even sound bone for long.
كذي وقرات كدن يكسرن عظمهولن تلبث العظم الصحيح القوارع
For I, in hoping to live when they have gone,am like one held back from an ascent, yet bound to climb it.
فإني وتأميلي الحياة، وقد مضواكمحتبس عن مطلع، وهو طالع
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: وقرات is understood provisionally as cracks or notches in bone; its precise sense here is uncertain.
  • Line 3: The image in محتَبِس عن مطلع وهو طالع is uncertain. I take مطلع as an ascent and وهو طالع as expressing the inevitability of climbing it, apparently an image of unavoidable death; the latter phrase could also mean 'while he is climbing.'

مقاس بن شريك، من الطويل

And Muqās ibn Sharīk ibn ʿAmr, an ally of the Banū Shaybān, said:

وقال مقاس بن شريك بن عمرو، حليف لبني شيبان:

I wept for Sharīk in al-Ghawār, and for Aswad,and Dhū al-ʿAlaq, until no moisture remained in my eyes.
بكيت شريكا في الغوار، وأسوداوذو العلق حتى ما بعيني من بلل
Men who held the first fruits of glory; there was no fearfor their neighbor of the blows of fate or reversals.
رجالا له ربعية المجد لم يخفمجاورهم ريب الحوادث والزلل
Through them we tended the whole community and fed onspring pasture, and relieved the debtor of the burden he bore.
وكنا بهم نرعى الجميع، ونأكل الربيع، ونكفي حامل الغرم ما حمل
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: Al-Ghawār is provisionally treated as a place name; I cannot identify it securely, and الغوار may instead refer to raiding. ذو العلق appears to be a personal epithet, but its expected accusative form here would be ذا العلق.
  • Line 2: The phrase له ربعية المجد is unclear and may be corrupt. The translation provisionally reads له as لهم, referring to the men, and takes ربعية المجد as an image of glory's early or first yield.
  • Line 3: نرعى الجميع is ambiguous: it may mean looking after the whole company or pasturing together. The translation adopts the former.

نهار بن توسعة، من الكامل

Nahār ibn Tawsiʿa said, elegizing his brother ʿItbān:

وقال نهار بن توسعة، يرثي أخاه عتبان:

ʿItbān, I was a man of strength,until I was bereft of you—and fortunes crumble.
عتبان قد كنت امرأ لي جانبحتى رزئتك، والجدود تضعضع
I was defiant of restraint, heedless;then I looked straight ahead, and my neck straightened.
قد كنت أشوس في المقادة سادرافنظرت قصدي واستقام الأخدع
And I lost my brothers, in whose lifetimeI could give what I wished and withhold.
وفقدت إخواني الذين بعيشهمقد كنت أعطي ما أشاء وأمنع
To whom can I say, when a calamity strikes,'Guide me with your counsel'? To whom can I turn?
فلمن أقول، إذا تلم ملمةأرني برأيك، أو إلى من أفزع؟!
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 2: فنظرت قصدي is elliptical; I take it to mean 'I looked straight ahead.' الأخدع literally denotes a neck vein, apparently standing here for the neck. The precise force of this image is uncertain, but it seems to contrast humbled obedience with former defiance.

البراء بن ربعي، من الطويل

And al-Barāʾ ibn Ribʿī said:

وقال البراء بن ربعي:

After my mother's sons, who followed one another in death,am I to hope for life, or dread death?
أبعد بني أمي الذين تتابعواأرجى حياة، أو من الموت أجزع؟!
Eight they were, the foremost of their people;through them I could give or withhold whatever I wished.
ثمانية كانوا ذؤابة قومهمبهم كنت أعطي ما أشاء وأمنع
They were like an archer's arrows in a quiver;then it was left without a single arrow.
وكانوا كنبل المرتمى في كنانةفأضحت وما فهيا من النبل أهزع
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 3: فهيا appears to be a typo for فيها, 'in it.' I take المرتمى to mean 'archer,' normally written المرتمي.

And Abū Kabīr al-Hudhalī said:

وقال أبو كبير الهذلي:

Many a man have I lowered into a grave,each in the first bloom of youth, well favored.
ولرب من طأطأته في حفرةمن كل مقتبل الشباب محبر
Then I turned away—I will not pour out my despair to you—trembling on my feet, swaying like a man with a bent neck.
ثم انثنيت، فلا أبثك حيبتيرعش القيام أميس ميس الأصور

هذيلة بن سماعة، من الطويل

And Hudhayla ibn Samāʿa ibn Ashwal said:

وقال هذيلة بن سماعة بن أشول:

A reproaching woman spent the night blaming me,while I lay as though grief were an adversary I wrestled.
وعاذلة باتت بليل تلومنيفبت كأن الهم قرن أجاذبه
I remembered Banū Sahl, with between me and themthe ravines of al-Ḥimā, its corners and its slopes.
ذكرت بني سهل وبيني وبينهمشراج الحمى أركانه ومناكبه
Is it true that I shall never meet Ziyād, nor seeQinān leading the cavalry, his locks disheveled?
أجدى لن ألقى زيادا، ولا أرىقنانا يقود الخيل شعثا ذوائبه
Nor see the like of those young men who passed away at Manʿij,swift when fear sends its rider galloping.
ولا مثل فتيان تولوا بمنعجعجالي إذا ما الخوف أوضع راكبه
Men—had the solid rocks on either flank of Qanāfallen from it as they fell, its sides would have collapsed.
رجالا لو أن الصم من جانبي قناهوت مثلهم منه لزلت جوانبه
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: شراج is understood as ravines or watercourses. الحمى may designate a particular place, provisionally rendered al-Ḥimā, or simply mean protected pastureland.
  • Line 3: أجدى appears to represent أجِدّي, an interrogative expression meaning approximately 'Is it really true?' The transmitted spelling is uncertain. The unvocalized personal name قنان is provisionally read Qinān.

الفرزدق ؟، من الطويل

And al-Farazdaq, Hammām ibn Ghālib, said, elegizing Hammām ibn Nāshira, one of the Banū ʿĀmir:

وقال الفرزدق، همام بن غالب، يرثي همام بن ناشرة، أحد بني عامر:

I stopped at my clan’s dwelling and was moved to tearsby the bareheaded women mourning their loss.
وقفت فأبكتني بدار عشيرتيعلى رزئهن الباكيات الحواسر
They went forth like Indian swords, entering a maelstromof death—an entry that allowed no return.
غدوا كسيوف الهند وراد حومةمن الموت أعيا وردهن المصادر
Defenders who protected their sacred charges and kept watchin the abode of death, with spears entangled.
محامين حاموا عن حريم، وحافظابدار المنايا، والقنا متشاجر
They seemed, beneath the fluttering banners, as they wenttoward death, like lions of the two thickets, crushing their prey.
كأنهم تحت الخوافق إذ غدواإلى الموت أسد الغابتين الهواصر
Had Salmā suffered a loss like ours,it would have crumbled; yet ʿĀmir bears the loss.
ولو أن سلمى نالها مثل رزئنالهدت، ولكن تحمل الرزء عامر
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 2: The wording أعيا وردهن المصادر is difficult. The translation takes المصادر as departures or returns, contrasting with ورود, entry or arrival. The feminine plural pronoun in وردهن may refer back to the swords, but its reference is uncertain.
  • Line 3: وحافظا is grammatically unclear after the plural حاموا and may be corrupted. It is translated provisionally as though وحافظوا ('and kept watch' or 'and guarded') were intended.
  • Line 4: الغابتين literally means 'the two thickets/forests'; it is unclear whether a particular locality is intended.

I have seen death come, and come again,to our house, its path to us easy.

My soul has been divided into two parts:one part is with the dead, and the other remains with me.

  • Line 1: The syntax of فقد بلغت إلا قليلا عروقها is uncertain. I understand it as saying that nearly all the tree’s roots have been reached, supplying death as the agent from the following verses.

  • Line 2: خذام appears to name a kinship group; its identification and vocalization are uncertain. Khidhām is provisional.

Poem

[Istaqalla]: rose up.

(استقل) : ارتفع

Death and doom were given power over them;their skulls lie amid the echoes of the graves.
سلط الموت والمنون عليهمفلهم في صدى المقابر هام
So shall every people end:the days will surely wear them away.
وكذاكم يصير كل أناسسوف حقا تبليهم الأيام
In their wake my soul wastes awayin sorrow upon sorrow; remembering them makes me ill.
فعلى إثرهم تساقط نفسيحسرات، وذكرهم لي سقام
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 1: The phrase «في صدى المقابر هام» is uncertain. I have rendered صدى as “echoes” and هام as “skulls,” but both words can also designate owls associated in ancient Arab belief with the dead; that imagery may be intended.

الشريف الرضى، من البسيط

And al-Sharīf al-Raḍī—may God be pleased with him—said:

وقال الشريف الرضي - رضي الله عنه -:

My father's sons, he has struck at you with all his weaponsand gained all he wished—this ever-youthful Fate.
بني أبي، قد رمى فيكم بشكتهونال ما شاء هذا الأزلم الجذع
You were stars shining beside al-Dahnāʾ,lighting nights wholly black and nights dark before moonrise.
كنتم نجوما لدى الدهناء زاهرةتضيء منها الدياجي السود والدرع
If your lights go out after they have rentthe robe of darkness—even dawn's light has an end.

هلال بن الأسعر، من الوافر

Hilāl ibn al-Asʿar said, elegizing a man of his people named al-Mughīra ibn Qanbar, who used to support him and treat him generously:

وقال هلال بن الأسعر، يرثي رجلا من قومه يقال له: المغيرة بن قنبر، كان يعوله ويفضل عليه:

Oh, would that al-Mughīra were alive,and death had consumed all people before him!
ألا ليت المغيرة كان حياوأفنى قبله الناس الفناء

ليبك على المغيرة كل خيلإذا أفنى عرائكها اللقاء
Let every helpless dependent weep for al-Mughīra,every poor man whom his giving sustained.
