أعرابي، من البسيط

Ibrāhīm ibn Adham—may God be pleased with him—said: “Of all I endured in giving up the things of this world, nothing was harder for me than parting from my homeland.” al-Riyāshī said: A Bedouin recited to me:

قال إبراهيم بن أدهم - رضي الله عنه: "ما قاسيت فيما تركت من الدنيا أشد علي من مفارقة الأوطان". قال الرياشي: أنشدني أعرابي:

Greet Qaṭan, if you know it well,with the greeting of one who once loved Qaṭan.
سلم على قطن إن كنت تألفهسلام من كان يهوى مرة قطنا

[Qaṭan]: a mountain.

(قطن) : جبل:

I love it—by Him who set its foundations firm—with a love that lies hidden when its signs appear.
أحبه والذي أرسى قواعدهحبا إذا ظهرت آياته بطنا
Would that we might never leave its grounds,and would that it were with us wherever we journeyed far from home.
يا ليته لا نريم الدهر ساحتهوليته حيث سرنا غربة معنا
No stranger, however much he shows himself steadfast,will fail to remember his homeland after living away.
ما من غريب وإن أبدى تجلدهإلا سيذكر بعد الغربة الوطنا
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: Qaṭan is probably the mountain of that name in Najd, though the identification is uncertain.
  • Line 2: The wording «إذا ظهرت آياته بطنا» is puzzling: literally, the love becomes hidden when its signs appear. It may mean that its full depth remains concealed despite its outward signs.

عبد الوهاب بن علي بن نصر، من الطويل

And the judge Abū Muḥammad ʿAbd al-Wahhāb ibn ʿAlī ibn Naṣr said:

وقال القاضي أبو محمد عبد الوهاب بن علي بن نصر:

I pine, forever recalling East and West,yet neither the land’s east nor its west is mine.
أهيم بذكر الشرق والغرب دائماوما لي لا شرق البلاد ولا الغرب
Rather, I long for homelands far away, and loved onesI have lost; whenever I recall our days together, I yearn.
ولكن أوطانا نأت وأحبةفقدت، متى أذكر عهودهم أصب
I do not forget the one I bade farewell to by the riverbank at dawn,when the camel-drivers sang out and the caravan hastened.
وما أنس من ودعت بالشط سحرةوقد غرد الحادون واستعجل الركب
Two close companions: one setting out into exile,the other remaining, his heart departed from his body.
أليفان: هذا سائر نحو غربةوهذا مقيم سار عن جسمه القلب
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: The unvocalized أصب is taken as a form of صبا, ‘to yearn’; a passive reading, ‘I am stricken,’ is also possible.
  • Line 3: أنس appears where the negative construction would normally require أنسى, ‘I forget.’ The final letter may have been dropped in transcription; the translation follows the apparent sense, ‘I do not forget.’

زامل بن عفير، من الخفيف

From Ibn al-Kalbī, who said: There was a man of Ṭayyiʾ called Zāmil ibn ʿUfair, who was staying among his maternal kinsmen of Kalb in former times, before the War of al-Fijār. A raiding party of Banū al-Qayn attacked them and drove off his camels. He appealed to his maternal kinsmen for help, but they were slow to respond. So he took a camel that had been set free as a sacred offering, mounted it bareback, and set out toward al-Shām. Someone said to him, “Will you ride what is forbidden?” He replied, “He who has nothing lawful rides what is forbidden.” As he drew near al-Shām, he passed a luxuriant meadow with pools of water. He hobbled his camel, ate some of the plants in that meadow, and lay down. While he lay there, a horseman approached the meadow, dismounted, removed his saddle, hobbled his horse, and sat down near where the man of Ṭayyiʾ was lying. The sound of his movements woke the man of Ṭayyiʾ, who sat upright. The horseman asked him, “Who are you?” He gave his lineage, and the horseman asked about his circumstances. He told him his story, and the horseman said, “My good man, have you any food? I have eaten nothing since yesterday.” He replied, “Do you ask for food when this meat is here before us?” Then he sprang to his sword and slaughtered his camel, cut off its hump, and opened its belly to take out its liver, all before the horseman’s eyes. He then lit a great fire, roasted the meat, and kept passing pieces to the horseman until he had finished. Before long, a cloud of dust rose, and there were horses approaching, jolting their riders as they came. When they reached the horseman, they greeted him with the salutation due to a king. He mounted and said, “Take charge of this man.” One of them took him up behind him, and they brought him to the royal residence. The horseman proved to be al-Ḥārith al-Akbar al-Ghassānī. He ordered one of his servants to provide lodging for the man of Ṭayyiʾ. Zāmil feared that the king had forgotten him, so he said to the servant, “Would you do me a kindness and convey to the king what I have to say?” He replied, “I will.” Then Zāmil recited to him:

عن ابن الكلبي قال: كان رجل من طيء يقال له: زامل بن عفير، نازلا في أخواله من كلب في الدهر الأول، وذلك قبل حرب الفجار، فأغار عليهم منسر من بني القين، فاستخفوا إبله، فاستنصر أخواله، فأبطئوا عليه، فعمد إلى جمل سائب، فاكتفله وتوجه نحو الشام، فقيل له: أتركب الحرام؟ قال: "يركب الحرام من لا حلال له" فلما قرب من الشام مر بروضة غناء وغدران، فقيد بعيره، وأكل من نبات تلك الروضة، واضطجع، فبينا هو كذلك، إذ أقبل فارس إلى الروضة، فنزل عن فرسه، وحط سرجه، وقيد فرسه، وقعد قريبا من مضطجع الطائي فاستيقظ الطائي بحرسه، فاستوى قاعدا، فقال له الفارس: من الرجل؟ فانتسب له، وسأله عن شأنه، فقص عليه قصته، فقال له الفارس: يا هذا. هل عندك طعام فإني طاو منذ أمس؟ فقال له: أتطلب الطعام وهذا اللحم معرض؟ ثم وثب إلى سيفه، فعقر بعيره، ثم اجتب سنامه، وبقر عن كبده، وذلك بعين الفارس، ثم أوقد نارا عظيمة، ثم اشتوى، وأقبل يلقى إلى الفارس، حتى انتهى، فما لبث أن ثار العجاج، فإذا الخيل مقبلة، تتوقص بفرسانها، حتى انتهوا إلى الفارس فحيوه بتحية الملك، فركب، وقال: دونكم الرجل، فأردفه بعضهم حتى أتى دار ملكه، فإذا هو الحارث الأكبر الغساني، فأمر بعض غلمانه بإنزال الطائي، وخاف زامل أن يكون قد نسيه الملك، فقال للغلام: هل لك أن توليني عارفة، وتبلغ الملك ما أقول؟ قال: أفعل، فأنشده:

Tell al-Ḥārith, rooted in gloryand noble deeds, forefather after forefather,
أبلغ الحارث المردد في المجد وفي المكرمات جدا فجد
and son of lords who trodthe broad way and ruled Ghawr and Najd:
وابن أرباب واطئ السبب الأرحب والمالكين غورا ونجدا
I look toward you, but between usstand obstacles that have turned my nearness into distance.
إنني ناظر إليك ودونيعائقات غادرن قربي بعدا
Though I lodge in a noble abode,at ease in my evening and morning pursuits,
إن أكن نازلا بمثوى كريمناعم البال في مراح ومغدى
yet a man is drawn to his homelandby longing, even if he lives there in hardship.
غير أن الأوطان يجتذب المرء إليها الهوى وإن عاش كدا
My lingering in al-Shām brings mesorrows that tear my heart to shreds.
وتأنى بالشآم مفيديحسرات يقددن قلبي قدا
A stranger finds no dwelling pleasantoutside his own land, even if he finds good fortune.
ليس يستعذب الغريب مقامافي سوى أرضه وإن نال جدا
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: المردد appears to describe glory repeatedly inherited through the ancestry. جدا فجد is understood as 'forefather after forefather'; the final word may be missing an alif.
  • Line 2: واطئ is singular as printed, although the surrounding construction seems to require plural واطئي, 'those who tread.' السبب الأرحب is tentatively rendered 'the broad way'; its precise reference is unclear.
  • Line 6: وتأنى is unclear. The translation provisionally reads it as وتأنّي, 'and my lingering,' but it may be corrupted from وتنائي, 'and my being far away.'

الشريف الرضي، من البسيط

And al-Sharīf al-Raḍī—may God be pleased with him—said:

وقال الشريف الرضي - رضي الله عنه -:

The sands are never mentioned but an exile yearns,whose heart’s desires and homes lie in Dhū al-Raml.
لا يذكر الرمل إلا حن مغتربله بذي الرمل أوطار وأوطان
My heart’s longings flutter toward the bān trees;it is not the bān trees that move me, but the one whose home is there.
يهفو إلى البان من قلبي نوازعهوما بي البان، بل من دارهالبان
I stop my ears when the doves sing there,lest an outcry betray the secret of my longing.
أسد سمعي إذا غنى الحمام بهاكيلا يبين سر الوجد إعلان
Many a dwelling I keep my distance from,though that dwelling stirs raptures and sorrows in me.
ورب دار أوليها مجانبةولي إلى الدار أطراب وأشجان
When I turn to gaze among its ruins, there rush forthwaters and fires for my heart and eye.
إذا تلفت في أطلالها ابتدرتللقلب والعين أمواه ونيران

  • Line 1: Dhū al-Raml is treated as a place name, but its location is unidentified; the expression could also describe a sandy tract.
  • Line 2: The joined text «دارهالبان» is read as «داره البان», ‘whose home is the bān trees.’ bān denotes the moringa tree, though al-Bān could here be a place name.

مصعب بن محمد ابن الفرات، من الطويل

One of the sages was asked, “What is pleasure?” He replied, “Having enough while remaining in one’s homeland and conversing with friends.” He was then asked, “What is humiliation?” He replied, “Being far from one’s homeland and wandering from land to land.” Abū al-ʿArab Muṣʿab ibn Muḥammad ibn al-Furāt said:

قيل لبعض الحكماء: ما اللذة؟ قال: الكفاية مع لزوم الأوطان، ومحادثة الإخوان، وقيل: فما الذلة؟ قال: النزوح عن الأوطان، والتنقل في البلدان. وقال أبو العرب مصعب بن محمد بن الفرات:

I ponder my course, with two resolves: one points east,while the other urges my ambition westward.
أهم ولى عزمان: عزم مشرقوآخر يغري همتي بالمغارب
I must ask the camels to fulfill a needthat will tax their hooves and withers.
ولا بد لي أن أسأل العيس حاجةتشق على أخفافها والغوارب
It falls to me to strive hopefully toward my aspirations,but the fulfillment of desires rests with fate.
علي لآمالي اضطراب مؤملولكن على الأقدار نجح المطالب
O my soul, do not take abasement as your companion: it is—though its inducements may deceive—
فيا نفس لا تستصحبي الهون إنهوإن خدعت أسبابه
the worst companion.
شر صاحب
O my homeland, if you part from me, Ishall make my home on the saddles of noble, fleet camels.
ويا وطني إن بنت عني فإننيسأوطن أكوار العتاق النجائب
If my origin is dust, then all the earth ismy homeland, and all the world's people are my kin.
إذا كان أصلي من تراب فكلهابلادي، وكل العالمين أقاربي

And another poet said:

وقال آخر:

O you who dwell far from your homeland,alone, weeping for your home!
يا بعيد الدار من وطنهمفردا يبكي على سكنه
Whenever his journey pressed him onward,the ailments in his body grew more severe.
كلما جد النجاء بهجدت الأسقام في بدنه
The heart’s sorrow was deepenedby a turtledove’s voice upon its branch.

شفه ما شفني فبكىكل من يبكي على شجنه
Translator's notes (4)
  • Line 1: سكن can mean a dwelling or someone in whom one finds comfort, especially a beloved; 'home' is chosen here.
  • Line 2: النجاء denotes swift travel or flight; the verse does not specify whether he is fleeing.
  • Line 3: شجي is read as شجى, 'sorrow,' with an apparent spelling or transcription irregularity.
  • Line 4: كل من يبكي على شجنه literally reads 'everyone who weeps over his sorrow.' Its syntax here is awkward and may reflect a textual error; the translation takes the intended sense to be that the speaker and dove each weep over their own sorrow.

Another poet said:

وقال آخر:

You see one who has made this world his home,not knowing that death will drive him from it.
ترى الذي اتخذ الدنيا له وطنالم يدر أن المنايا عنه تزعجه
Whoever knows that death is his path,the grave his dwelling, and resurrection his departure,
من كان يعلم أن الموت مدرجهوالقبر منزله، والبعث مخرجه
and that he faces either gardens that will delight himon the Day of Resurrection, or a fire that will roast him—
وأنه بين جنات ستبهجهيوم القيامة، أو نار ستنضجه
then everything but piety ill becomes him,and whatever else he persists in is ugliest of all.
فكل شيء سوى التقوى به سمجوما أقام عليه فهو أسمجه
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 4: The final hemistich is compressed and ambiguous, particularly the pronoun in أسمجه. I take ما أقام عليه to mean whatever conduct other than piety he persists in, with persistence making it especially unseemly.

Another poet said:

وقال آخر:

My homes are among the homelands laid waste;two lifetimes of mine have passed among thriving dwellings.
من الخراب من الأوطان أوطانيوقد مضى لي في العمران عمران
I blamed the first to turn my reins away fromthe paths of passion, and rebuked the second who turned me back.
قد لمت أول من يثني عناني عنسبل الهوى وزجرت الثاني الثاني
In a gathering whose sight delights those who behold it,I have two sweet companions from Ḥulwān.
في مجلس تبهج الرائين رؤيتهولي قرينان من حلوان حلوان
They speak of no one's shortcomings in my presence,nor, when my hands give generously, do they restrain me.
لا يحضران امرءا عندي بمنقصةولا إذا جادت الكفان كفاني
But separation from a slender-waisted one clouds my life;his ceaseless swagger, sleeves swinging, has undone me.
لكن يكدر عيشي بعد ذي هيفإدمانه الخطر بالأردان أرداني
His glances say: “Look! Held withinmy eyelids are the likes of what scabbards hold.”
تقول ألحاظه: أنظر، فقد ضمنتأشباه ما ضمت الأجفان أجفاني
He shunned me, though he never used to, and left medead; yet, if he wished, he could sometimes bring me back to life.
جفا، وما كان يجفوني وغادرنيميتا، ولو شاء في الأحيان أحياني
Cruel fate has set his dwelling far from my abode;had our two homes been close, he would have treated me kindly.
والحين أبعد عن مأواي مسكنهولو تقاربت الداران داراني
An envious man, emboldened by my distance, has angered me;had our lands adjoined, he would have sought to please me.
وحاسد غره بعدي فأسخطنيولو تجاورت الأرضان أرضاني
How many an envious man has been consumed by what he sees ofmy merit; how many a hater has choked with grief at my fortunes!
كم قد وري من حسود ما يعاين منفضلي، وكم قد شجى من شأني شاني
Translator's notes (4)
  • Line 1: I read عمران as the dual of عُمر, “two lifetimes,” in wordplay with العمران, “inhabited or flourishing places”; the intended force of the dual is uncertain.
  • Line 3: Ḥulwān is a place-name borne by several localities; which one is intended is uncertain.
  • Line 4: لا يحضران امرءا عندي بمنقصة literally suggests bringing someone before the speaker with a fault attributed to him. I take it to mean that the companions do not disparage others in his presence.
  • Line 10: وري appears to be وَرِيَ, referring to an internal consuming disease or ulceration, here caused figuratively by envy. “Has been consumed” reflects this reading.
