كعب بن الأشرف، من الكامل

Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf was killed in Rabiʿ al-Awwal, twenty-five months after the Hijra. Ibn Ishaq—may God have mercy on him—said: [One went] to the people of al-Safila, and ʿAbd Allah ibn Rawaha—may God have mercy on him—to the people of al-ʿAliya, as bearers of good news. The Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—had sent the two of them to the Muslims in al-Madina with news of the victory God Most High had granted him and of those polytheists who had been killed. When the news reached Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf—who was a man of Tayyiʾ, specifically of Banu Nabhan, while his mother was of Banu al-Nadir—he said: “Is this true? Do you think Muhammad has killed those whom these two men are naming?”—meaning Zayd ibn Haritha and ʿAbd Allah ibn Rawaha, may God be pleased with them both. “These are the nobles of the Arabs and the kings of men! By God, if Muhammad has struck down these men, the earth’s belly is better than its surface.” When the enemy of God became certain of the news, he set out and came to Makka, where he stayed with al-Muttalib ibn Abi Wadaʿa ibn Subayra al-Sahmi, whose wife was ʿAtika, daughter of Abu al-ʿIs ibn Umayya ibn ʿAbd Shams ibn [ʿAbd] Manaf. Al-Muttalib lodged him and treated him generously. He then began inciting people against the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—reciting poems and lamenting the men of Quraysh who lay in the well, those who had been slain at Badr. Among his verses were these:

كان قتل كعب بن الأشرف في ربيع الأول، على رأس خمسة وعشرين شهرا من الهجرة. قال ابن إسحاق - رحمه الله - إلى أهل السافلة، وعبد الله بن رواحة - رحمه الله - إلى أهل العالية بشيرين، بعثهما رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى من بالمدينة من المسلمين بفتح الله تعالى عليه، وقتل من قتل من المشركين، قال كعب بن الأشرف - وكان رجلا من طيء، ثم أحد بني نبهان، وكانت أمه من بني النضير - حين بلغه الخبر: أحق هذا؟ أترون محمدا قتل هؤلاء الذين يسمى هذان الرجلان؟ - يعني زيد بن حارثة، وعبد الله بن رواحة رضي الله عنهما - فهؤلاء أشراف العرب وملوك الناس، والله لئن كان محمد أصاب هؤلاء القوم لبطن الأرض خير من ظهرها، فلما تيقن عدو الله الخبر خرج حتى أتى مكة، فنزل على المطلب بن أبي وداعة بن صبيرة السهمي، وعنده عاتكة ابنة أبي العيص بن أمية بن عبد شمس بن [عبد] مناف، فأنزله وأكرمه، فجعل يحرض على رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم، وينشد الأشعار، ويبكي أصحاب القليب من قريش الذين أصيبوا ببدر، فمن ذلك قوله:

Badr’s mill ground its people to destruction;for such a day as Badr, tears pour forth.
طحنت رحى بدر لمهلك أهلهاولمثل بدر تستهل الأدمع
The chiefs of men were killed around their watering pools;may you not be lost to us! Even kings are struck down.
قتلت سراة الناس حول حياضهملا تبعدوا إن الملوك تصرع
How many a fair-faced, noble man was struck down there,a man of radiant presence, with whom the destitute found refuge,
كم قد أصيب به من ابيض ماجدذي بهجة تأوي إليه الضيع
open-handed when the stars failed to bring rain,a bearer of burdens, a leader held in honor.
طلق اليدين إذا الكواكب أخلفتحمال أثقال يسود ويرفع
And some people say—[their remoteness stirs me?]—“Ibn al-Ashraf, Kaʿb, is still grieving.”
ويقول أقوامأثير بشحطهم إن ابن الأشرف ظل كعب يجزع
They speak the truth. If only, the hour they were killed,the earth had kept sinking with its people and splitting apart.
صدقوا، فليت الأرض ساعة قتلواظلت تسوخ بأهلها وتصدع
May the man who relayed the news for a morsel [unclear],or live blind from failing sight, unable to hear.
صار الذي أثر الحديث بطعمةأو عاش أعمى من عشا لا يسمع
I have been told that all the Banū al-Mughīrabowed in grief at the killing of Abū al-Ḥakīm and were brought low.
نبئت أن بني المغيرة كلهمخشعوا لقتل أبي الحكيم وجدعوا

[Abu al-Hakim]: he means Abu Jahl.

يعني [بأبي الحكيم] : أبا جهل

I have been told that their al-Ḥārith ibn Hishāmbuilds up good deeds among the people and gathers his forces,
نبئت أن الحارث بن هشامهمفي الناس يبنى الصالحات ويجمع
to visit Yathrib with his hosts; onlythe gallant defend noble lineage.
ليزور يثرب بالجموع، وإنمايحمي عن الحسب الكريم الأروع
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 5: أثير بشحطهم is unclear. شحط means distance or remoteness; “their remoteness stirs me” is only a tentative reading of the supplied wording, which may be corrupt. The hemistich division also appears displaced: the first hemistich contains only ويقول أقوام.
  • Line 7: صار الذي أثر الحديث بطعمة does not yield a clear construction. أثر الحديث can mean “relayed the report,” and بطعمة could mean “for a morsel,” but their connection with صار is unresolved. The translation tentatively treats the line as a curse, suggested by “or live blind … unable to hear”; the missing sense has not been supplied.
  • Line 8: The text gives أبي الحكيم, hence Abū al-Ḥakīm. This probably refers to Abū al-Ḥakam, better known as Abū Jahl, who was killed at Badr; الحكيم may be a misprint for الحكم.

Then Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf returned to al-Madīna and composed love verses about Umm al-Faḍl bint al-Ḥārith, saying:

ثم رجع كعب بن الأشرف إلى المدينة، فشبب بأم الفضل بنت الحارث، فقال:

Are you departing without having stopped at Muntabih,and leaving Umm al-Faḍl in al-Ḥaram?
أراحل أنت لم تلمم بمنتبهوتارك أنت أم الفضل بالحرم؟
All between her ankles and her elbows quiverswhen she slowly attempts to rise, yet does not stand.
ترتج ما بين كعبيها ومرفقهاإذا تأنت قياما ثم لم تقم
She is the very likeness of Umm Ḥakīm when we were lovers,and the bond with her was strong, unbroken.
أشباه أم حكيم إذ تواصلناوالحبل منها متين غير منجذم
A woman of Banū ʿĀmir—my heart is madly in love with her;if she wished, she could cure Kaʿb of his sickness.
إحدى بني عامر هام الفؤاد بهاولو تشاء شفت كعبا من السقم
Foremost among women, her father foremost among his people—people of the encampment, faithful to their pledges.
فرع النساء، وفرع القوم والدهاأهل المحلة والإيفاء بالذمم
Never before her had I seen a sun rise at night,until she shone upon us in the deepest darkness.
لم أر شمسا بليل قبلها طلعتحتى تجلت لنا في ظلمة الظلم
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: منتبه is taken provisionally as a place name, Muntabih. I cannot identify the place or establish its vocalization; the spelling may be corrupt.
  • Line 3: أشباه literally means 'likenesses'; its plural form and grammatical connection are unclear here. I have understood it as comparing the woman to Umm Ḥakīm, whom I cannot identify from the supplied context.

Then he composed amorous verses about the Muslim women, causing offense to the Muslims. The Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—said, “Who will deal with Ibn al-Ashraf for me?” Muḥammad ibn Maslama [a member of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal]—God have mercy on him—said, “I will deal with him for you, Messenger of God. I will kill him.” He said, “Then do so, if you can.” Muḥammad went back and spent three days neither eating nor drinking except enough to keep himself alive. This was mentioned to the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—who summoned him and asked, “Why have you given up food and drink?” He replied, “Messenger of God, I gave you a promise, but I do not know whether I can fulfill it for you.” He said, “You are responsible only for making the effort.” He said, “Messenger of God, we shall have to say things.” He replied, “Say whatever seems necessary to you; you are absolved of blame for it.” Those who joined together to kill him were Muḥammad ibn Maslama; Abū Nāʾila Silkān ibn Salāma ibn Waqsh, who was Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf’s foster-brother; ʿAbbād ibn Bishr ibn Waqsh; al-Ḥārith ibn Aws ibn Muʿādh; and Abū ʿAbs ibn Jabr—God be pleased with them. Before going to Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf themselves, they sent Abū Nāʾila ahead to him. He came and conversed with him for a while, and they recited poetry to one another, for Abū Nāʾila composed poetry. Then he said, “Listen, Ibn al-Ashraf, I have come to you with a need that I wish to tell you about, so keep it confidential.” He said, “I shall.” He said, “This man’s arrival among us has been a calamity: the Arabs have become our enemies and have shot at us as though from a single bow. The roads have been cut off from us, so that our dependents are destitute and we ourselves are exhausted. We and our dependents are now in dire straits.” Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf said, “By God, Ibn Salāma, I used to tell you that things would turn out just as I said.” Abū Nāʾila said to him, “There are some men among my companions who share my opinion. I would like to bring them to you so that we may buy food from you. Treat us generously in this, and we shall give you a pledge sufficient to secure what you are owed.” Kaʿb said, “Abū Nāʾila, I would not have wished to see you in such poverty. You are indeed among the people dearest to me: you are my brother, who shared the breast with me. Tell me honestly what is in your heart: what do you intend to do about him?” He said, “Abandon him and withdraw from him.” He said, “You have delighted me, Abū Nāʾila. What will you pledge to me? Will you pledge your sons?” He replied, “You would disgrace us!” He said, “Will you pledge your women?” He replied, “How could we pledge our women to you, when you are the most youthful and fragrant of the people of Yathrib? Instead, we shall pledge you enough arms to secure what you are owed.” Abū Nāʾila intended that he should not be suspicious of their weapons when they brought them. He said, “Arms will indeed provide sufficient security.” Abū Nāʾila left him with an appointment arranged. They agreed to go to him that evening at the appointed time. Then they came to the Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace—at nightfall and informed him. Ibn Isḥāq said: The Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—walked with them as far as al-Baqīʿ, then sent them on their way, saying, “Go in the name of God. O God, help them.” Then the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—returned to his house. They proceeded until they reached his fortress, and Abū Nāʾila called out to him. Kaʿb had recently married. He sprang up in his wrap, but his wife caught its edge and said, “You are a man at war, and men at war do not go down at this hour.” He said, “It is Abū Nāʾila. If he found me asleep, he would not wake me.” She said, “By God, I recognize evil in his voice.” He replied, “Were a young man summoned to a spear-thrust, he would answer.” He went down and talked with them for a while, and they talked with him. Then they said, “Ibn al-Ashraf, would you like us to walk together to Shiʿb al-ʿAjūz and spend the rest of this night talking there?” He said, “If you wish.” They set out walking together. After they had walked for a while, Abū Nāʾila put his hand into the locks on either side of Kaʿb’s head, then smelled his hand and said, “Never have I encountered perfume more fragrant than tonight’s.” He walked a little farther, then said, “Strike the enemy of God!” They struck him, but their swords crossed over him and accomplished nothing. Muḥammad ibn Maslama said: When I saw that our swords were accomplishing nothing, I remembered a dagger concealed in my sword and took it out. The enemy of God had let out a cry, and there was not a fortress around us on which a fire had not been lit. I set the dagger against his lower abdomen and bore down upon it until I reached his pubic region. The enemy of God fell, and I cut off his head. Al-Ḥārith ibn Aws ibn Muʿādh had been wounded in the leg, struck by one of our swords. He said: We left and climbed up into the lava field of al-ʿUrayḍ. Our companion al-Ḥārith ibn Aws had fallen behind us and was weakened by loss of blood. We waited for him a while until he came to us, following our tracks. We carried him and brought him to the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—[at the end of the night]. Al-Wāqidī said: When they reached Baqīʿ al-Gharqad, the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—had risen and was standing at the door of the mosque. He said, “May these faces prosper!” They replied, “And yours, Messenger of God!” They cast Kaʿb’s head before him, and he praised God for his killing. Then they brought their companion al-Ḥārith, and he spat upon his wound, which thereafter caused him no pain. Muḥammad ibn Maslama said: By morning the Jews were afraid because of our attack upon the enemy of God; there was not a Jew among them who did not fear for his own life. Al-Wāqidī said: The Jews and the polytheists allied with them were alarmed. In the morning they came to the Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace—and said, “Our companion, one of our chiefs, was attacked last night and treacherously killed, without any crime or offense that we know of.” The Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace—said, “Had he remained quiet, as others who share his views have remained quiet, he would not have needed washing for burial. But he inflicted harm upon us and lampooned us. Any one of you who does as he did will meet the sword.” The Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—called upon them to draw up a written agreement between them, whose terms they would observe. They drew up an agreement between themselves and him—God bless him and grant him peace—in the house of Ramla bint al-Ḥadath. From the day Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf was killed, the Jews were afraid. I say: The verse called for an account of the killing of Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf, so I have related it, although it was not among the matters I set out to treat. His words—mighty and majestic is He—“For the poor emigrants who were driven from their homes and their possessions”—and the rest of the verse. By “the emigrants,” the Exalted means those Muslims who left their homelands for the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—in the abode of emigration, namely al-Madīna, out of fear of harm from the polytheists and a desire to support His Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace. They are those foremost in Islam. “Seeking bounty from God and His good pleasure”: that is, bounty from the gifts of God Most High in this world, and His good pleasure through His reward in the hereafter. ʿAlī ibn Rabāḥ al-Lakhmī related that ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb—God’s good pleasure be upon him—delivered a sermon at al-Jābiya, saying: “Whoever wishes to ask about the Qurʾān should go to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb. Whoever wishes to ask about the prescribed shares of inheritance should go to Zayd ibn Thābit. Whoever wishes to ask about jurisprudence should go to Muʿādh ibn Jabal. Whoever wishes to ask about public funds should come to me, for God Most High has appointed me their keeper and distributor. I shall begin with the wives of the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—and give them their shares, then with the first emigrants, my companions: we were driven from our homes and our possessions.” Qatāda said: Because they chose God and His Messenger—God bless him and grant him peace—despite the hardships they endured. Indeed, we have been told that a man would bind a stone against his belly to keep himself upright from hunger, and that in winter a man would make a hollow in the ground his covering, having no other covering. May God’s good pleasure be upon them all. And His words—mighty and majestic is He—“Nūḥ said, ‘My Lord, do not leave upon the earth a single dweller among the unbelievers. If You leave them, they will lead Your servants astray and beget none but wicked, inveterate unbelievers.’” There are two interpretations of His word “dayyāran”: one is that it means anyone at all; the other is someone who inhabits dwellings. It is said that a man from the people of Nūḥ—peace be upon him—passed by him carrying a young son on his shoulder. He said to his son, “Beware of this man, for he will lead you astray,” meaning Nūḥ—peace be upon him. The boy said to his father, “Put me down.” He took him down from his shoulder, and the boy threw something at Nūḥ—peace be upon him—and gashed his head. Then Nūḥ—peace be upon him—grew angry and prayed against them. It is also said that he prayed against them when God—mighty and majestic is He—revealed to him: “None of your people will believe except those who have already believed.” From Abū Burayda, from his father—God have mercy on him—who said: The Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—used to teach them, when they entered graveyards, that one of them should say: “Peace be upon you, inhabitants of these abodes, believers and Muslims. We shall indeed join you. We ask God for well-being for ourselves and for you.” Al-Khanūt, who is Tawba ibn Muḍarris, said:

ثم شبب بنساء المسلمين حتى آذاهم، فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "من لي من ابن الأشرف"، فقال محمد بن مسلمة [أخو بني عبد الأشهل]- رحمه الله - أنا لك به يا رسول الله، أنا أقتله، قال: فافعل إن قدرت على ذلك، فرجع محمد، فمكث ثلاثا لا يأكل ولا يشرب إلا ما يعلق به نفسه، فذكر ذلك لرسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم، فدعاه، فقال له: لم تركت الطعام والشراب؟ قال: يا رسول الله قلت لك قولا لا أدري أوفي لك به أم لا، قال: إنما عليك الجهد، قال: يا رسول الله إنه لابد أن نقول قال: "قولوا ما بدا لكم؛ فأنتم في حل من ذلك" فاجتمع في قتله محمد بن مسلمة، وأبو نائلة سلكان ابن سلامة بن وقش، وكان أخا كعب بن الأشرف من الرضاعة، وعباد بن بشر بن وقش، والحارث بن أوس بن معاذ، وأبو عبس بن جبر رضي الله عنهم، ثم قدموا إلى كعب بن الأشرف - Her people also had dealings with Banu al-Nadir, and they came to them while he was staying among them. Salma told her people, “He will take me away before the sacred month ends.
