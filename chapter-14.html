On the house (dār)

فصل في ذكر الدار

أنشدها علي بن محمد بن ثابت، من الكامل

It is related from Yazīd ibn al-Aṣamm that the Ansar—may God be pleased with them—said: “O Messenger of God, divide the land into two halves between us and our brothers among the Muhājirūn—may God be pleased with them.” He—God bless him and grant him peace—said: “No. Rather, you shall spare them the burden of its upkeep and share the fruit with them, while the land remains yours.” They said: “We are content.” Then God, mighty and exalted, revealed: “Those who had settled in the abode and in faith before them love those who have emigrated to them, find no desire in their hearts for what they have been given, and prefer them to themselves, even though they themselves are in need.” As for His words, blessed and exalted is He, “They shall have the Abode of Peace with their Lord,” this is Paradise. There are two explanations for its being called the Abode of Peace. The first is that it is the abode of perpetual safety from every affliction. The second is that al-Salam is God—glory be to Him—and Paradise is His abode. There are two explanations of His words, exalted is He, “with their Lord.” The first is that the Abode of Peace is with their Lord in the hereafter, because it belongs more particularly to Him. The second is that they have a promise with their Lord that He will lodge them in the Abode of Peace. The same expression occurs in His words, exalted is He: “God calls to the Abode of Peace.” Of His words, mighty and exalted is He, “The abode of the hereafter is better; how excellent is the abode of the God-fearing,” it has been said that the hereafter is better than this world because this world passes away while the hereafter endures. Regarding “How excellent is the abode of the God-fearing,” al-Ḥasan—may God be pleased with him—said: “Excellent is this world as an abode for the God-fearing, for through their deeds in it they attained the reward of the hereafter and entry into Paradise.” Concerning His words, blessed and exalted is He, relating the story of Qārūn, “Then We caused the earth to swallow him and his dwelling,” Ibn ʿAbbās—may God be pleased with him—said: “Mūsā—peace be upon him—complained to God, mighty and exalted, about Qārūn, so God Most High commanded the earth to obey Mūsā. When Qārūn and his followers approached, Mūsā said: ‘O earth, seize them!’ It seized them up to their heels. Then he said: ‘Seize them!’ It seized them up to their waists. Then he said: ‘Seize them!’ It seized them up to their necks. Then he said: ‘Seize them!’ And it swallowed them, together with Qārūn’s dwelling and his treasures.” Yazīd al-Raqāshī—may God have mercy on him—related that when the earth had seized Qārūn up to his neck, Mūsā—peace be upon him—took his sandals and struck him in the face with them. Qārūn said: “O Mūsā, have mercy on me!” God Most High said: “O Mūsā, how hard your heart is! My servant called upon you and begged you for mercy, but you did not have mercy on him. By My might, had he called upon Me, I would have answered him.” Samura ibn Jundab related that Qārūn and his people are made to sink into the earth by the height of a man each day, and that he will not reach the lowest earth until the Day of Resurrection. Muqātil said: “When Mūsā—peace be upon him—commanded the earth and it swallowed Qārūn, the Banū Isrāʾīl said: ‘He destroyed him only to inherit his wealth,’ for he was the son of Mūsā’s paternal uncle, his father’s brother. So, after three days, God Most High caused the earth to swallow his dwelling and all his possessions.” His words, mighty and exalted is He, “Who, of His bounty, has lodged us in the abode of lasting residence,” mean the abode of residence, which is Paradise. There are two explanations of the distinction between muqama, with a u-vowel, and maqama, with an a-vowel. The first is that with a u-vowel it means the abode of residence, and with an a-vowel the place of residence. The second is that with a u-vowel it means a gathering place where people assemble for food, and with an a-vowel a gathering place where people assemble for conversation. His words, exalted is He, “Those who had settled in the abode and in faith before them,” refer to the Ansar—may God be pleased with them—who settled in al-Madina before the Muhājirūn emigrated there. It has been said that they settled in the abode before them, and in faith after them; and it has been said that they settled in the abode and in faith before the emigration to them. “They love those who have emigrated to them”: by sharing their possessions and dwellings with them. “And find no desire in their hearts for what they have been given”: that is, no envy of the property from the fayʾ that was allotted exclusively to them. “And prefer them to themselves, even though they themselves are in need”: this means that they favor them and put them before themselves, even though they suffer poverty and want. There are two opinions concerning their preference for them. The first is that they preferred them to themselves in the distribution of the fayʾ and spoils that had been obtained, so that these were divided among the Muhājirūn to their exclusion. It is related that the Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace—distributed among the Muhājirūn the property of al-Naḍīr—or, according to another account, Qurayẓa—that God Most High had granted as fayʾ. The Ansar—may God be pleased with them—said: “Rather, we shall give them a share of our own possessions and give them preference in the fayʾ.” Then God, mighty and exalted, revealed this verse. The second opinion is that they favored the Muhājirūn—may God be pleased with them—with their possessions and shared them with them. Ibn Zayd related that the Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace—said to the Ansar: “Your brothers have left behind their possessions and children and come out to you.” They said: “Our possessions shall be divided among them as allotments.” He—God bless him and grant him peace—said: “Or shall it be otherwise?” They said: “How would that be, O Messenger of God?” He said: “They are people unfamiliar with the work. Will you spare them that burden and share the dates with them?” He meant the dates from the palm trees of the Banū al-Naḍīr that had come into their possession. They said: “Yes, O Messenger of God.” “And those who are guarded against the greed of their own souls—it is they who shall prosper.” It has been said that shuhh means wanting for oneself what other people possess. It has also been said to mean withholding the zakat; following the soul’s desires; or acquiring what is unlawful. Al-Aswad related from Ibn Masʿūd—may God be pleased with him—that a man came to him and said: “I fear that I am ruined.” He said: “Why is that?” The man said: “I have heard God, mighty and exalted, say, ‘And those who are guarded against the greed of their own souls—it is they who shall prosper,’ and I am a miserly man, scarcely letting anything leave my hand.” Ibn Masʿūd—may God have mercy on him—said: “That is not the greed God Most High mentions in the Quran. The greed God Most High mentions in the Quran is to consume your brother’s property unjustly. What you describe is miserliness, and an evil thing miserliness is.” It is related from the Prophet—God bless him and grant him peace: “How utterly astonishing is one who believes in the abode of everlasting life, yet works for the abode of delusion!” From Abū al-Dardāʾ—may God be pleased with him—who said: “The Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—addressed us one Friday, saying: ‘O people, repent before you die, and hasten to perform righteous deeds before you become preoccupied. Maintain the bond between yourselves and your Lord, and you will be happy. Give abundant charity, and you will be provided for. Enjoin what is right, and you will enjoy abundance. Forbid what is wrong, and you will be granted victory. O people, the wisest among you are those who remember death most often, and the most prudent among you are those best prepared for it. Indeed, among the signs of understanding are withdrawing from the abode of delusion, turning toward the abode of everlasting life, laying in provisions for dwelling in the graves, and preparing for the Day of Resurrection.’” ʿAlī ibn Muḥammad ibn Thābit al-Kātib recited:

The Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib—may God be pleased with him—delivered a sermon in al-Kūfa, saying in the course of his address: “Glory be to You, Creator and object of worship! How graciously You deal with Your creatures! You created an abode and provided a banquet, food and drink, spouses, palaces, servants, springs, and rivers. Then You sent a summoner to us, but we neither answered the summoner nor desired what You encouraged us to desire. We turned instead to a carcass, feeding upon it, each striving to outdo the others in greed for it. We disgraced ourselves by agreeing together in loving it. The eyes of our righteous men and our jurists have been blinded concerning it and for its sake. Whoever has a sickness in his heart looks with an unsound eye and listens with an ear that cannot hear. Desires have taken possession of his reason, this world has put his heart to death, and his soul has become heedlessly engrossed in it. He is its slave, and the slave of anyone who holds any part of it in his hands. Wherever it moves, he moves with it; whenever it approaches, he turns toward it. He neither understands nor hears. No warning from God restrains him, and no admonition from God teaches him a lesson. “He has seen those seized unawares, when there is neither release nor return: how those terrors surprised them, how what they had been promised descended upon them, how they left their dwellings and passed into their graves, and how they encountered the calamities of those events. When anguish for their own souls descends upon their hearts, two things come together upon them: regret for what has been lost and the stupor of death. Their hearts are rent by them, their complexions change, their gasps come repeatedly, and they move their hands and feet as their souls depart, so that their foreheads break into sweat. Then death gains further upon them, and each is prevented from speaking, though he is still among his own people. With what reason remains to him, he reflects: on what was his lifetime spent, and on what did his days pass away?” It is related from al-Aṣmaʿī—may God have mercy on him—who said: “I went on pilgrimage and stopped at Ḍariyya on a Friday. There was a Bedouin who had wound his turban and slung his bow over his shoulder. He ascended the pulpit, praised and extolled God, invoked blessings upon His Prophet—may God bless him and grant him peace—and then said: “‘O people! This world is an abode of passage, and the hereafter an abode of permanence. Take, then, from your abode of passage provisions for your abode of permanence. Do not tear away your veils before Him from whom your secrets are not hidden. No one meets a new day of his life without parting with another day of his allotted term. Yesterday is an admonition, today an opportunity to gain, and tomorrow—no one knows who will live to see it. Set right what you are advancing toward, and expend what you will not return to. Take your hearts out of this world before your bodies leave it. In it you were created, but to another abode you were summoned. No strong being is stronger than the Creator, and no weak being is weaker than a creature. There is no flight from God except to Him. How can anyone flee when he turns about in the hands of the One who pursues him? “‘“Every soul shall taste death, and you will be paid your rewards in full only on the Day of Resurrection. Whoever is drawn away from the Fire and admitted to the Garden has indeed triumphed. The life of this world is nothing but the enjoyment of delusion.”’” It is related from ʿĪsā ibn Maryam—God’s blessings be upon him—that he said: “Who would build a house upon the waves of the sea? Such is this world, so do not make it a permanent abode.” It is related from Wahb ibn Munabbih—may God have mercy on him—who said: “ʿĪsā ibn Maryam—God’s blessings be upon him—said: ‘O abode, you will fall into ruin, and your inhabitant will perish! O soul, work, and you will be provided for! O body, toil, and you will find rest!’” A man said to al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī—may God be pleased with him: “Abū Saʿīd, when I am hungry I grow weak, and when I am full I am overcome by breathlessness.” He replied: “My nephew, this abode does not suit you, so seek another.” It is related from Ṣāliḥ al-Murrī—may God have mercy on him—who said: “When al-Manṣūr grew angry with al-Muryani and demolished his house, I entered it one day to walk about and take warning. A black man came out to me from one of the chambers and said: ‘This is a creature’s wrath. What, then, of the Creator’s wrath against a creature?’” It is related from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī—may God be pleased with him—that he said: “This world is an abode of work. Whoever keeps company with it while hating it, renouncing it, and holding it in low esteem will prosper through it, and his companionship with it will benefit him. Whoever keeps company with it while desiring and loving it will be made wretched by it. It will diminish his portion with God Most High, then deliver him over to that which he has no endurance to bear: God’s punishment and wrath. Its importance is slight, its enjoyment little, and its passing away decreed. God is the inheritor of it, and its people move on to dwellings that do not decay and are not altered by the passage of time. Their lifespan there does not run out so that they die, nor, however long their stay, will they depart. Beware, then—and there is no strength save through God—of that dwelling place, and often recall that destination.” Ibn Muṭīʿ‘ looked at his house and admired its beauty. Then he wept and said: “By God, were it not for death, I would rejoice in you! Were it not for the narrow graves to which we are bound, our eyes would find delight in this world.” Then he wept until his voice rose aloud. وكنا عهدنا الدار والدار مرةهي الدار إذ حلت بها أم يعمرا
There I recalled old times, despite estrangement and disdain;and the sorrowful must needs remember.
ذكرت بها عهدا على الهجر والقلىولابد للمحزون أن يتذكرا

عمر بن أبي ربيعة، من الطويل

And ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa al-Makhzūmī said:

وقال عمر بن أبي ربيعة المخزومي:

Is it by a dwelling’s faded trace that you stand,on a plain where raging winds efface it?
أفي رسم دار دارس أنت واقفبقاع تعفيه الرياح العواصف
I stood there; none whom I question speaks,nor, though the dwelling does not speak, do I turn away.
وقفت بها، لا من أسائل ناطقولا أنا إذ لم تنطق الدار صادف
Nor am I unmindful of those who frequented the dwelling;nor is my lovesickness dispelled, nor does my heart turn away.
ولا أنا عمن يألف الدار ذاهلولا التبل مردود ولا القلب عازف
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 3: التبل can mean rancor or a claim for vengeance, but here I take it as the affliction caused by love. The precise sense of التبل مردود is uncertain.

حارثة بن بدر، من البسيط

And Ḥāritha ibn Badr al-Ghudānī said:

وقال حارثة بن بدر الغداني:

Greet the dwelling, deserted after long ages,a waste on the outskirts of upper Dhāt Imhād.
سلم على الدار أقوت بعد آبادقفرا بطارف أعلى ذات إمهاد
Nothing remained of the dwelling when I came upon itbut the places where tent ropes and pegs had been set.
الدار لم يبق منها ريثما لقيتإلا مضارب أطناب وأوتاد
In the wilderness, when it came into view, it seemed,from the valley of Dhāt al-Sanā, like tattered cloaks.
كأنها بالفيافي يوم مطلعهامن بطن ذات السنا أخلاق أبراد
I could not make it out until I stopped thereand long swept my gaze downward and upward.
فما تبينتها حتى وقفت بهاوطال بالطرف إفراعي وإصعادي
Recognizing it, my eye poured down tears,like water-drawers splashing abundant water from unfailing wells.
فانهلت العين من عرفانها سكبانضح السقاة لجم ماء أعداد
So I lingered, like a drinker overcome by wine,a whole long day upon a sturdy she-camel and her saddle-frame.
فظلت كالشارب النشوان محتبسايوما طويلا على عنس وأقتاد
I let my gaze wander wearily, then turned it backover winter quarters, summer quarters, and places we frequented—
أراسل الطرف وهنا ثم أعطفهفي متشتى ومصطاف ومرتاد
when distance did not part our families,nor were our hidden secrets laid bare.
إذ لا النوى بين أهلينا مفرقةولا المكتم من أسرارنا باد
Translator's notes (4)
  • Line 1: آباد is taken as the plural of أبد, 'ages.' Dhāt Imhād is treated as a place name, but its identification and vocalization are uncertain.
  • Line 2: ريثما لقيت is syntactically awkward. The translation provisionally takes it as 'when I came upon it,' with an implied object; the transmitted wording may be defective.
  • Line 3: The relation of يوم مطلعها ('when it appeared') to من بطن ذات السنا ('from the valley of Dhāt al-Sanā') is uncertain: the latter may locate the observer rather than the ruins. Dhāt al-Sanā has not been identified.
  • Line 7: وهنا is taken as 'wearily,' but it could instead be a temporal expression meaning 'late at night.'

من الكامل

Another poet said:

وقال آخر:

An abode whose splendor surpasses the dwellings of kings,as its master surpasses kings.
دار علا دور الملوك بهاؤهاكعلو صاحبها على الأملاك
So beautiful and lofty, it is as thoughits foundations were laid upon the celestial spheres.
فكأنها من حسنها وعلوهابنيت قواعدها على الأفلاك

رجل من عبس، من الكامل

A man of ʿAbs—one of the people of al-Kūfa who had moved to live among his maternal kin, Juʿf—said:

وقال رجل من عبس - من أهل الكوفة تحول إلى أخواله جعف -:

When my home no longer suited me, I turned away from it;I left it and settled among Juʿf.
لما نبت داري نبوت بهافتركتها ونزلت في جعف
There I settled in honor and esteem,in a place between the eye and the nose.
فحللت في عز وتكرمةبمحل بين العين والأنف

This derives from the saying of the Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace: “ʿAmmār is the skin between my eyes and my nose,” meaning ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, may God have mercy on him.

هذا من قول النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: "عمار جلدة ما بين عيني وأنفي" يعني عمار بن ياسر رحمه الله.

Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 1: جعف apparently denotes the tribe of Juʿfī in a shortened form, transliterated here as Juʿf.
