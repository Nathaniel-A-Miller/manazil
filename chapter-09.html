أبو العتاهية، من الكامل

Abū al-ʿAtāhiya said:

قال أبو العتاهية:

They hoarded, but did not eat what they hoarded;they built their dwellings, but did not dwell in them.
جمعوا فما أكلوا الذي جمعواوبنوا مساكنهم فما سكنوا
It was as though they were a caravan there:once they had rested a little while, they departed.
وكأنهم كانوا بها ظعنالما استراحوا ساعة ظعنوا

أبو العلاء المعرى، من الطويل

It is related of Abū al-Dardāʾ’—may God be pleased with him—that he looked out upon the people of Ḥimṣ and said: “Have you no shame? You build what you will not inhabit, hope for what you will not attain, and amass what you will not eat! Where are those before you who built lofty buildings, amassed great wealth, and cherished far-reaching hopes? Their dwellings have become graves, their hopes delusions, and their accumulated wealth a ruin.” And Abū [al-ʿAlāʾ’ Aḥmad ibn ʿAbd Allah] ibn Sulaymān al-Maʿarrī said:

روى عن أبي الدرداء - رضي الله عنه - أنه أشرف على أهل حمص فقال: "ألا تستحيون؟ تبنون ما لا تسكنون، وتأملون ما لا تدركون، وتجمعون ما لا تأكلون! أين الذين بنوا قبلكم تشييدا، وجمعوا كثيرا، وأملوا بعيدا،؟ أصبحت مساكنهم قبورا، وآمالهم غرورا، وجمعهم بورا". وقال أبو [العلاء أحمد بن عبد الله] بن سليمان المعري:

I dwelt in you, O world, unwilling and compelled,and I had neither hand nor say in that.
سكنتك يا دنيا برغمي مكرهاوما كان لي في ذاك صنع ولا أمر
I tried you until I knew you through and through:you are a vessel filled with sorrow and sin.
وجربت حتى قد قتلتك خبرةفأنت وعاء حشوه الهم والوزر
If one day I depart, I shall leave you despised,with neither sapling nor seed of my stock left in you.
فإن أرتحل يوما أدعك ذميمةوما فيك من عودي غراس ولا بذر

النابغة الذبياني، من الطويل

It is related of Ḥātim al-Aṣamm—may God have mercy on him—that he said: “Not a morning passes without Satan saying to me, ‘What will you eat? What will you wear? And where will you dwell?’ I reply, ‘I shall eat death, wear a shroud, and dwell in the grave.’” al-Nābigha al-Dhubyānī said:

عن حاتم الأصم - رحمه الله - أنه قال: ما من صباح إلا والشيطان يقول لي: ما تأكل؟ وما تلبس؟ وأين تسكن؟ فأقول؛ آكل الموت، وألبس الكفن، وأسكن القبر. وقال النابغة الذبياني:

Laylā has dwellings to the east of al-Nijād,desolate, effaced by the north wind and persistent rain.
لليلى بشرقي النجاد مساكنقفار تعفتها شمال وداجن
I faintly discerned a familiar spot among them, then recognized it,after seven years—and this year is the eighth.
توهمت منها معهدا، فعرفتهلسبعة أعوام وذا العام ثامن
They endured time’s vicissitudes, but [separation carried]Laylā far away from your people’s home.
أقامت على ريب الزمان وأعجتبليلى نوى عن دار أهلك شاطن
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: النجاد may be a place name, rendered here as al-Nijād, or simply the plural of najd, meaning uplands.
  • Line 3: The form أعجت is unclear and may be corrupt. The bracketed rendering is conjectural, based on the following phrase بليلى نوى عن دار أهلك شاطن, which describes a departure or separation taking Laylā far from the addressee’s people.

كثير بن عبد الرحمن، من الطويل

And Kuthayyir ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān said:

وقال كثير بن عبد الرحمن:

I visited Laylā’s dwellings at al-Burūd,grown old, with the ages sweeping over them.
غشيت لليلى بالبرود مساكناتقادمن واستنت عليها الأعاصر
They lay desolate after their people left, save for dwellingsthat look newly made, though they are falling into ruin.
وأوحشن بعد الحي إلا مساكنايرين حديثات وهن دوائر
When they grew grassy and their dwelling-ground flourished,a company of your beloved’s people would be staying there.
وكانت إذ أخلت وأمرع ربعهايكون عليها من صديقك حاضر
Now the tribe has departed after dwelling there,and nothing is there but the wandering winds.
فقد خف منها الحي بعد إقامةفما إن بها إلا الرياح العوائر
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: The precise identification and vocalization of البرود, rendered al-Burūd, are uncertain.
  • Line 2: The exception إلا مساكنا, 'save for dwellings,' is awkward because the preceding subject is itself the dwellings. The wording may be defective; it is translated as transmitted.
  • Line 3: أخلت is taken here to mean 'grew grassy,' from الخلى, fresh herbage, rather than 'became empty.' The phrase من صديقك حاضر is also ambiguous: صديقك may mean 'your friend' or 'your beloved,' and حاضر may denote either a settled company or someone present.

البحتري، من الكامل

And al-Buḥturī said:

وقال البحتري:

May the clouds water your dwelling, and may there always beabundant rain there, and deep-red blossoms.
أسقى محلتك الغمام، ولا يزالنوء بها خضل، ونور جاسد
For I once knew life beneath its shadeLuxuriant, its harvest praised by the scout.
فلقد عهدت العيش في أفيائهافينان يحمد مجتناه الرائد
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: جاسد is an uncommon epithet for blossoms; translated as “deep-red,” though the precise color intended is uncertain.
  • Line 2: الرائد ordinarily means a scout seeking pasture and water. Here it appears within the image of life as luxuriant growth yielding a harvest.

البحتري، من الكامل

He also said:

وقال أيضا:

Say to the clouds, when the north wind drives them onwardand their departing caravan travels by night:
قل للسحاب إذا حداه الشمألوسرى بليل ركبه المتحمل
Turn aside to Ḥalab and water a quarterinhabited and welcoming, where ʿAlwa has a home.
عرج على حلب فسق محلةمأنوسة، فيها لعلوة منزل
A guileless girl’s home—I draw near in love, while she draws away;I lavish my treasured affection, while she withholds hers.
لغريرة أدنو وتبعد في الهوىوأجود بالود المصون وتبخل
I incline tenderly toward you, with anguish in my heart;I turn away from you, yet the face of my love turns toward you.
أحنو عليك وفي فؤادي لوعةوأصد عنك ووجه ودي مقبل
I stand proud, then abase myself with a lover’s abasement;for love holds both pride and humility.
وأعز ثم أذل ذلة عاشقوالحب فيه تعزز وتذلل
Whenever I think of union with another, I am held backby yearning for you, and an earlier advocate pleading for you.
وإذا هممت بوصل غيرك ردنيوله عليك وشافع لك أول
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 6: The referent of “an earlier advocate” (شافع لك أول) is unspecified; it may mean the speaker’s prior love for her, which argues against a new attachment.

البحترى، من الكامل

Al-Buḥturī also said:

وقال البحتري أيضا:

O two abodes of Salmā at Kāẓima, may you remain unharmed;and know that love is what you have stirred.
أمحلتي سلمى بكاظمة اسلماوتعلما أن الهوى ما هجتما
Can you slake the thirst of one seeking his loved ones,or help a love-stricken man bear his longing?
هل ترويان من الأحبة حائماأو تسعدان على الصبابة مغرما
I weep tears for you both; were I to weepin proportion to my love, I would weep blood for you.
أبكيكما دمعا، ولو أني علىقدر الهوى أبكى بكيتكما دما
A ruin where I hold back tears eloquentwith burning grief, and read a script that cannot speak.
طللا أكفكف فيه دمعا معربابجوى، وأقرأ فيه خطا أعجما
Its hillocks refuse to answer; yet no one questionedcould answer before he understood.
تأبى رباه أن تجيب، ولم يكنمستخبر ليجيب حتى تفهما
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 4: طللا appears to be the accusative singular ṭalalan, “a ruin,” but its grammatical connection to the preceding address to two abodes is unclear.

And al-Sharīf al-Murtaḍā—may God be pleased with him—said:

وقال الشريف المرتضى - رضي الله عنه -:

البحترى، من الطويل

And al-Buḥturī said:

وقال البحتري:

A dwelling at al-Qāṭūl, whose once-inhabited quarters have decayed,and time’s vicissitudes have become an army raiding it.
محل من القاطول أخلق عامرهوعادت صروف الدهر جيشا يغاوره
As though the east wind fulfills vows when it journeys by night,its trailing skirts visit the place at evening and at dawn.
كأن الصبا توفى نذورا إذا سرتتراوحه أذياله وتباكره
Many a season there was gentle in its course,its edges soft, its verdure bursting into leaf.
ورب زمان ناعم ثم عهدهترق حواشيه ويورق ناضره
Al-Jaʿfarī’s beauty and convivial life have changed;its desert folk and townspeople have struck camp.
تغير حسن الجعفري وأنسهوقوض بادي الجعفري وحاضره
Its inhabitants departed suddenly,and its houses and graves became alike.
تحمل عنه ساكنوه فجاءةفعادت سواء دوره ومقابره
Whenever we visit it, it renews our grief,though before this day it used to delight its visitor.
إذا نحن زرناه أجد لنا الأسىوقد كان قبل اليوم يبهج زائره
I have not forgotten the palace’s wild creatures when their herd was startled,when its fawns and wild-cow calves were terrified.
ولم أنس وحش القصر إذ ريع سربهوإذ ذعرت أطلاؤه وجآذره
And when the call to depart rang out within it, and they tore awayin haste its curtains and hangings.
وإذ صيح فيه بالرحيل فهتكتعلى عجل استاره وستائره
And its desolation—as though it had never helda friendly soul, nor its vistas ever pleased the eye.
ووحشته حتى كأن لم يكن بهأنيس، ولم تحسن لعين مناظره
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: The masculine pronoun in أذياله (‘its trailing skirts’) is unexpected with الصبا (‘the east wind’), normally feminine. The translation takes the skirts to belong to the wind.
  • Line 7: The palace’s ‘wild creatures,’ ‘fawns,’ and ‘wild-cow calves’ apparently stand metaphorically for its women or young inhabitants; the precise referents are not explicit.

أبو تمام، من الطويل

Abū Tammām said:

قال أبو تمام:

Stop, and renew your acquaintance with the familiar haunts,though they do not heed a seeker's inquiry.
قفوا جددوا من عهدكم بالمعاهدوإن هي لم تسمع لنشدان ناشد
The desolate dwelling has bowed its head at their lossand their departure, like one bereaved, mourning the dead.
لقد أطرق الربع المحيل لفقدهموبينهم إطراق ثكلان فاقد
Time's way with them has given it deadly poison to drink;the venom of nights surpasses the venom of black serpents.
سقته ذعافا عادة الدهر فيهموسم الليالي فوق سم الأساود
Within it burns an anguish of parting, deaf and unresponsiveto healing, yet calling forth no visitor to tend it.
به غلة للبين صماء لم تصخلبرء، ولم توجب عيادة عائد

البحتري، من الكامل

And al-Buḥturī said:

وقال البحتري:

May rain water your old haunts, which made me yearn,and the site of your dwelling, which made me weep.
سقيت معاهدك اللواتي شقننيومحل منزلك الذي أبكاني
If I stay, my mind is journeying;or if I travel freely, my heart is captive.

الشريف الرضى، من الكامل

Al-Sharīf al-Murtaḍā—may God be pleased with him—said:

وقال الشريف المرتضى - رضي الله عنه -:

O haunts of loved ones, is there a return toa morning haunt where longing is cooled, and an evening resting-place?
أمعاهد الأحباب هل عود إلىمغدى يبل به الجوى ومراح
Our sighs and tears suffice youfor rain and breezes after we are gone.
يكفيك من أنفاسنا ودموعناأن تمطري من بعدنا وتراحي
May the clouds’ pouring rain and copious flow water al-Liwā,and may a downpour of wine refresh those dwelling there.
فسقى اللوى صوب الغمام ودرهوسقى النوازل فيه صوب الراح
How often life there had a gentle breeze,like water lying thin along the sides of broad valleys.
فلرب عيش رق فيه نسيمهكالماء رق على جنوب بطاح
Translator's notes (2)
  • The attribution is inconsistent: the poet field names al-Sharīf al-Raḍī, while the introduction names al-Sharīf al-Murtaḍā.
  • Line 2: I take the uncommon form «تراحي» to mean being swept by breezes, pairing the lovers’ breaths with wind and their tears with rain.

Abū al-Ṭayyib al-Mutanabbī said:

قال أبو الطيب المتنبي:

Let me blame myself if, at the time of reproach,I knew what ailed me among those landmarks.

We trod their soil with our camels’ feet;may I never cease seeking healing by kissing their footpads.

أبزون العماني، من الطويل

The chief Abū ʿAlī Abzūn al-ʿUmānī said:

وقال الرئيس أبو علي أبزون العماني:

The landmarks undo the knots of my endurance,and my eyes’ tears reveal what I conceal.
تحل عقود الصبر مني المعالموتبدي دموع العين ما أنا كاتم
And they blot out all trace of solace when they appear—the remnants of the tribe’s dwellings, themselves effaced.
وتطمس آثار العزاء إذا بدترسوم ديار الحي وهي طواسم
They were emptied of human gazelles, then took in their placegazelles, while my heart remains lovesick for those replaced.
خلت من طباء الإنس ثم تبدلتظباء، وقلبي بالمبدل هائم
They try to turn me away from Sālim, and I turn them back;yet Sālim is the skin between my eye and nose.
يديروني عن سالم وأديرهوجلدة بين العين والأنف سالم
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 3: طباء appears to be a misprint for ظباء, ‘gazelles,’ here used of beautiful people. المبدل is understood as those replaced, rather than their replacements.
  • Line 4: The first hemistich appears textually defective. The translation tentatively reads يديرونني for يديروني and وأديرهم (‘and I turn them’) for وأديره (‘and I turn him’). Sālim’s identity and the precise allusion are uncertain.

And Abū ʿAbd Allah Muḥammad ibn ʿUthmān, known as Ibn al-Ḥaddād, said:

وقال أبو عبد الله محمد بن عثمان المعروف بابن الحداد:

Ah, those are the landmarks among her hills;how can the eye hold back its tears?
ألا إنها الأعلام من هضباتهافكيف تكف العين عن عبراتها
Leave me, you two, to shed tears; perhaps theywill calm what memories of her have stirred.
ذراني وإذراء الدموع لعلهاتسكن ما قد هاج من ذكراتها
There I knew idols of beauty that knew mein my passion, as a worshipper of their ʿUzzā and their Manāt.
'هدت بها أصنام حسن عهدتنيهوى عبد عزاها، وعبد مناتها
I cry aloud my longings for her, and revereher laws in love with the reverence they are due.
أهل بأشواقي إليها وأتقيشرائعها في الحب حق تقاتها
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 3: The opening «'هدت» appears corrupted; I provisionally read عهدت, “I knew.” The grammatical role of هوى in the second hemistich is uncertain; “in my passion” is tentative.
  • The feminine pronouns are not explicitly anchored: they may refer to the beloved, her dwelling, or, in the later lines, the “idols of beauty.” The translation uses “her” except where “their” refers directly to those idols.

And Ibn Hāniʾ al-Maghribī said:

Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: The camels are described as hobbled, yet also as moving at two different gaits. The intended relation between these descriptions is unclear; 'yet' makes the apparent contrast explicit.
  • Line 3: أسلوا appears to be an irregular spelling or transcription of أسلو, 'I forget' or 'I cease to long.' It is translated here as a rhetorical question, parallel to أهجركم, 'Shall I forsake you?'

الفرزدق، من الوافر

And al-Farazdaq said:

وقال الفرزدق:

Will you not turn aside for us, that we maysee the deserted grounds or the traces of the tents?

Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: لعنا is understood as a dialectal form of لعلنا, ‘that perhaps we may.’
  • Line 2: عرضت is uncertain in this context; it may be a misprint for عرجت, ‘you turn aside,’ which is the sense adopted here. فأغن عنا is understood as asking the speaker to do enough weeping on the others’ behalf.
