On the land (arḍ)

فصل في ذكر الأرض
Index 499, page 208
وجيهة بنت أوس الضبية، من الطويل

Wajīha bint Aws al-Ḍabbiyya said:

قالت وجيهة بنت أوس الضبية:

A woman comes each morning to reproach mefor my longing; she has not erased yearning from my heart.
وعاذلة تغدو علي تلومنيعلى الشوق لم تمح الصبابة من قلبي
What fault is mine if I love my people's landand hate the tamarisks of al-Quṣayba?
فما لي إن أحببت أرض عشيرتيوأبغضت طرفاء القصيبة من ذنب
If a wind could convey the words of a senderfull of affection, I would confide in the south wind at al-Naqb.
ولو أن ريحا بلغت وحي مرسلحفي لناجيت الجنوب على النقب
And I would say to her: “Carry my message to them,and do not mingle it—may your good fortune endure—with dust.”
وقلت لها: أدى إليهم رسالتيولا تخلطيها طال سعدك بالترب
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 2: The place القصيبة, provisionally vocalized al-Quṣayba, has not been securely identified.
  • Line 3: I read مرسل حفي as 'a sender full of affection'; the unvocalized مرسل could alternatively mean 'messenger.'
  • Line 3: النقب is treated here as the place name al-Naqb, but it could instead mean 'the mountain pass.'

وقالت علية بنت المهدي:

An exile at al-Marj weeps in his grief,for those who comforted him in love have departed.

إذا ما أتاه الركب من نحو أرضهتنفس يستشفى برائحة القرب

وقال قيس بن ذريح:

It is not your land that I have loved, butI kiss the footprints of the one who trod its dust.

من الطويل

Another poet said:

وقال آخر:

I see that every land she has dwelt in—even asyears pass over it—has ever more fragrant soil.
أرى كل أرض دمنتها وإن مضتلها حجج يزداد طيبا ترابها
Do You not know, O Lord, how many a prayerI have offered You sincerely—if only He had answered it!
ألم تعلمن يا رب، أن رب دعوةدعوتك فيها مخلصا لو أجابها
I swear, if I could regard as kin to herthe wolves of the wild, those wolves would be dear to me.
فأقسم لو أني أرى نسبا لهاذئاب الفلا حبت إلى ذئابها
By the life of Laylā’s father, if she now dwellsin Wādī al-Qurā, her absence has harmed no one but me.
لعمر أبي ليلى لئن هي أصبحتبوادي القرى ما ضر غيري اغترابها
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: The unvowelled دمنتها permits several readings, including ‘I dwelt in it,’ ‘you dwelt in it,’ and ‘she dwelt in it.’ I have chosen the last in light of the subsequent references to the beloved.
  • Line 2: The wording is uncertain: لو أجابها literally means ‘if he had answered it,’ shifting from addressing God to the third person and supplying no explicit consequence. I have provisionally read it as a wish, ‘if only He had answered it’; the text may be defective.
  • Line 3: I read حبت إلى as حَبَّت إليَّ, ‘would be dear to me’; the printed إلى (‘to’) appears erroneous. The construction نسباً لها ذئاب الفلا is unusual; I understand it to mean regarding the wolves as kin to the beloved.

قعنب بن أم صاحب، من البسيط

And Qaʿnab ibn Umm Ṣāḥib said:

وقال قعنب ابن أم صاحب:

I had ceased to yearn for Najd, but I was stirredto remember it by al-Khaffān and al-Ḥuḍan.
قد كنت أقصرت عن نجد فهيجنيعلى تذكرها الخفان والحضن
When I came to a land I did not know,and the memory of another stirred my longing, sorrow rose within me.
لما وردت بلادا لست أعرفهاوشاقني ذكر أخرى هاج لي حزن
I said: “It is time, from the land where I was born,to return; and time for separation’s vicissitudes to yield reunion.
فقلت: قد حان من أرض ولدت بهاأوب، وقد حان من صرف النوى قرن
Perhaps your wanderings will one day bring you back; fora foreign land draws near, until home draws near.”
عل النوى بك يوما أن تريع، فقدتدنو الغريبة حتى يدنو الوطن
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: The vocalization and identification of الخفان, rendered al-Khaffān, are uncertain.
  • Line 3: The text says من أرض ولدت بها, “from the land where I was born,” although the context seems to require a return to that land. The wording may be corrupt. The second hemistich is also difficult: قرن is provisionally understood as “joining, reunion,” and صرف النوى as the vicissitudes of separation.
  • Line 4: The referent of الغريبة is not explicit; “a foreign land” assumes an omitted أرض. The precise sense of its drawing near before the homeland does remains uncertain.

أبو نصر بن النحاس، من المتقارب

And Abū Naṣr ibn al-Naḥḥās al-Ḥalabī said:

وقال أبو نصر بن النحاس الحلبي:

May God water a land where we clothed ourselves in youththroughout its reaches, and cast off solemnity.
سقى الله أرضا لبسنا الشبابأرجائها وخلعنا الوقارا
Dwellings that lamented the parting of those who journeyed awaythrough their colors, and adorned themselves with yellow flowers.
ديارا شكت فرقة الظاعنين بألوانها فتحلت بهارا
A time whose company we found sweet—it passed, leaving nothing but remembrance.
زمان صحبناه مستعذبافبان، ولم يبق إلا ادكارا
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: The word الشباب appears to be divided across the hemistichs, but a ب is apparently missing at the join. The translation assumes الشباب بأرجائها, “youth throughout its reaches.”
  • Line 2: بهار denotes a flowering plant, often identified as a yellow oxeye or a kind of narcissus; the precise botanical identification is uncertain.

من الطويل

Another poet said:

وقال آخر:

I exchanged Dūdān for Ḥamal and its land,but neither my drink nor my drinking place pleased me.
تعوضت من دودان حملا وأرضهافما طاب لي شربي ولا راق مشربي
If my fortunes were bound to Dūdān, I would not be assailed,whether I were guilty or innocent.
فإن تلتبس حلى بدودان لا أرملئن كنت ذا ذنب، وإن غير مذنب
Surely a man's own people are better protectionthan the farthest stranger, even if they are not near.
ألا إن قوم المرء خير تفيةمن الأبعد الأقصى، وإن لم تقرب
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: The identities of دودان and حمل are uncertain; Dūdān and Ḥamal are provisional vocalizations. Dūdān may designate a tribe rather than a place.
  • Line 2: حلى is unclear and may be corrupt. 'My fortunes' provisionally assumes حالي ('my condition'). لا أرم is read as لا أُرْمَ ('I would not be assailed'); another possible reading is لا أَرِمْ ('I would not depart'). The construction لئن كنت ... وإن غير مذنب is also awkward as transmitted; the translation takes it to mean 'whether guilty or innocent.'
  • Line 3: تفية is unclear, probably a misprint for تقية ('protection'), adopted in the translation. The subject and vocalization of تقرب are uncertain: 'even if they are not near' is provisional; 'even if you are not welcomed' is another possible interpretation.

أبو العلاء المعري، من الطويل

Abū al-ʿAlāʾ [Aḥmad ibn ʿAbd Allah] ibn Sulaymān said:

قال أبو العلاء [أحمد بن عبد الله] بن سليمان:

Depart in the morning from the ailing land,and accept no treatment for an intractable disease but excision.
تحمل عن الأرض المريضة غادياولا ترض للداء العياء سوى الحسم
A man's spirit is ever beset by calamities,grappling with them until it breaks free of the body.
وما فتئت روح الفتى في نوائبتمارسها حتى استقلت عن الجسم
When we part, we are rid of harm,and the shepherd pasturing his flock has no need to mark them.
إذا ما تفرقنا خلصنا من الأذىولم يحتج الراعي المسيم إلى الوشم
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: الحسم can mean cutting off or decisive termination, and can also refer to cauterization. 'Excision' preserves the force of cutting away, though the precise treatment intended is uncertain.
  • Line 3: الوشم ordinarily means tattooing. Here it apparently means marking livestock for identification, though الوسم would be the more usual word for branding animals. The precise connection between this pastoral image and 'when we part' is uncertain.

الشنفرى، من الطويل

And al-Shanfarā said:

وقال الشنفرى:

On earth the noble have a refuge from harm,and those who seek estrangement have somewhere else to go.
وفي الأرض منأى للكريم عن الأذىوفيها لمن رام القلى متحول
By your life, the earth is not too narrow for a manwho travels by night, in hope or fear, with his wits about him.
لعمرك ما بالأرض ضيق على امرئسرى راغبا أو راهبا وهو يعقل
I keep putting hunger off until I weary it,and turn my thoughts away from it until I forget.
أديم مطال الجرع حتى أملهوأضرب عنه الذكر صفحا فأذهل
I swallow the earth’s dust, lest he think he hasa claim of favor over me—some bestower of favors.
واستف ترب الأرض كيلا يرى لهعلى من الطول امرؤ متطول
But for my avoidance of reproach, no drink would be foundto sustain life, nor food, except in my possession.
ولولا اجتناب الذام لم يلف مشربيعاش به إلا لدى ومأكل
But an unyielding soul will not let me remainunder oppression longer than it takes me to depart.
ولكن نفسا مرة لا تقيم بيعلى الضيم إلا ريثما أتحول
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 3: الجرع ('swallowing') appears to be a misprint for الجوع ('hunger'), which is translated here. أمله is read as 'I weary it'; without vocalization, it could also mean 'I grow weary of it.'
  • Line 4: على appears to be a misprint for عليّ ('over me'), the reading used here.
  • Line 5: لدى is understood as لديّ ('in my possession' or 'with me').

On the authority of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAbd Allah ibn ʿUmar ibn Ḥafṣ ibn ʿĀṣim ibn ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb—may God be pleased with him—that a man of Banū Kilāb, known by the kunyah Abū Ḥibāl, stayed with ʿAbd Allah ibn ʿUmar ibn Ḥafṣ, accompanied by his son Ḥibāl. His son fell ill and then died. ʿAbd Allah said: “My father ordered that he be shrouded, so we shrouded him. When we had finished, his father asked my father’s permission to go in to him, greet him, and bid him farewell. He gave him permission, and he went in and bent over him. We heard him say:”

عن عبد الرحمن بن عبد الله بن عمر بن حفص بن عاصم بن عمر بن الخطاب - رضي الله عنه - أن رجلا من بني كلاب يكنى أبا حبال نزل على عبد الله بن عمر بن حفص، ومعه ابنه حبال، فمرض ابنه ثم مات، قال عبد الله: فأمر أبي بكفنه، فكفناه، فلما فرغنا منه استأذن أبوه أبي أن يدخل عليه، فيسلم عليه ويودعه، فأذن له، فدخل، فأكب عليه، فسمعناه يقول:

Were it not for Ḥibāl, my mount would never have knelt with mein a land of fevers, recurring and fierce.
ولولا حبال لم تنخ بي مطيتيبأرض بها الحمى بورد وصالب
A woman said, “By God, your love for him has ruined you!”My life for Ḥibāl—what a dear friend and companion!

Translator's notes (2)
  • The name حبال is unvocalized; its rendering as Ḥibāl is uncertain.
  • Line 1: ورد and صالب appear to designate types of fever. They are rendered as “recurring” and “fierce,” though the precise medical distinction is uncertain.

Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 2: The syntax of على رأي عيني ناظر واستماعه is unusual. I take عيني ناظر as ‘a beholder’s two eyes’ and the phrase as indicating that the speaker’s people are within sight and hearing.
  • Line 4: I read خليت as khallaytu, ‘I left,’ and معصما as muʿṣiman, ‘seeking or finding refuge.’ The wording is compressed, and the precise relationship between leaving the person and leaving one’s flourishing life with him is uncertain.
  • Line 5: نية الدار is interpreted as the distance or separation of the dwelling. The construction with غلبتني ... على is compressed; I understand it to mean that distance did not prevent the patron’s gifts and favor from reaching the speaker.

Translator's notes (3)
