On the house (bayt)

فصل في ذكر البيت
Index 821, page 359; partial quotation
من الطويل

God, blessed and exalted, said: “Indeed, the first House established for humankind was the one at Bakka, blessed and a guidance for all peoples.” Mujāhid—may God have mercy on him—said: The Muslims and the Jews disputed their respective claims to superiority. The Jews said, “Bayt al-Maqdis is better,” while the Muslims said, “Rather, the Kaʿba is better.” So God Most High revealed this verse. The scholars of historical traditions do not disagree that it was the first House established for humankind, but they differ over whether any other house had been built before it, holding two opinions. The first is that there had been many houses before it; this was the opinion of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib—God’s good pleasure be upon him—and al-Ḥasan—may God be pleased with him. The second is that no house had been built before it; this was the opinion of Mujāhid and Qatāda. It is related from Abū Dhikr—may God be pleased with him—that he said: I asked the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—“Which house was first established on earth?” He said, “Al-Masjid al-Ḥarām.” I asked, “Then which house?” He said, “Bayt al-Maqdis.” I asked, “How much time was there between them?” He said, “Forty years.” God, blessed and exalted, said: “And when Ibrāhīm was raising the foundations of the House, together with Ismāʿīl.” The first person after Adam whom God, mighty and majestic, guided to the site of the House was Ibrāhīm—peace be upon them both. He was the first to build it, together with Ismāʿīl—peace be upon him—and the first to make pilgrimage to it. Before him, people had merely prayed in its direction without knowing its site. They differ over the reason for its construction at the beginning of creation, holding two opinions. The first is what Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī related from his father—God’s good pleasure be upon them both—that God Most High placed beneath the Throne a house upon four pillars and named it al-Ḍurāḥ; this is al-Bayt al-Maʿmūr. God Most High said to the angels—peace be upon them—“Circumambulate it.” Then He sent His angels—peace be upon them—saying, “Build Me a house on earth in its likeness and of its dimensions,” and commanded those of His creatures who were on earth to circumambulate it. The second is what is related from Ibn ʿAbbās—God’s good pleasure be upon them both—who said: When Adam—peace be upon him—was sent down from Paradise to earth, God Most High said to him, “Adam, go and build Me a house. Circumambulate it and remember Me around it, as you saw the angels doing around My Throne.” Adam—peace be upon him—set off, striding along. The earth was folded up for him, and the deserts were drawn together for him. Wherever his foot fell became inhabited land, until he reached the site of al-Bayt al-Ḥarām. Jibrīl—peace be upon him—struck the earth with his wing, exposing a foundation firmly set upon the seventh and lowest earth. The angels cast rocks to him, each rock more than thirty men could manage. He built it from five mountains: Lubnan, Ṭūr Zaytā, Ṭūr Sina, al-Jūdī, and Ḥirāʾ. Thus Adam—peace be upon him—was the first to lay the foundations of the House, pray within it, and circumambulate it. It remained so until God Most High sent the Flood, and the site of the House was effaced. Then God, mighty and majestic, sent Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl—peace be upon them both—and they laid the foundations of the House and set up its boundary markers. Later, Quraysh built it. It stands directly beneath al-Bayt al-Maʿmūr: if that House were to fall, it would fall upon this one alone. Then ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib—God’s good pleasure be upon him—related that when God Most High commanded Ibrāhīm—peace be upon him—to build the House, he left al-Shām with his son Ismāʿīl and the boy’s mother Hājar. God sent with him the Sakīna, which had a tongue with which it spoke. Ibrāhīm would set out in the morning when it set out, and travel in the evening when it travelled, until it brought him to Makka. It said to Ibrāhīm, “Lay the foundation where I am.” He and Ismāʿīl—peace be upon them both—raised the House until they reached the place of the Corner. Then he said to his son, “My son, find me a stone to place upon it as a marker for the people.” He brought a stone, but Ibrāhīm was not satisfied with it and said, “Find me another.” He went off searching, and when he returned, the Corner Stone had been brought to Ibrāhīm, who set it in its place. Ismāʿīl said, “Father, who brought you this stone?” He replied, “One who did not leave me dependent on you, my son.” When Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl—peace be upon them both—raised the foundations of the House, a square cloud with a head within it came and called out, “Build upward following my square outline.” The judge al-Mārūdī—may God have mercy on him—said: This is what the transmitted accounts tell of the House before and after the Flood. Ibn Isḥāq—may God have mercy on him—said: When the Messenger of God—God bless him and grant him peace—reached the age of twenty-five, Quraysh assembled to rebuild the Kaʿba. They had been contemplating this so that they could roof it, but they dreaded demolishing it. It was merely stones piled up a little higher than a man’s height, and they wanted to raise it and put a roof over it. This was because a group of men had stolen the treasure of the Kaʿba, which was kept in a well inside it. The man in whose possession the treasure was found was Duwayk, a client of Banū Mulayḥ ibn ʿAmr of Khuzāʿa, and Quraysh cut off his hand. It was also said that those who stole it had deposited it with Duwayk. The sea had cast ashore at Judda a ship belonging to a merchant of al-Rūm, and it had broken apart. They took its timber and prepared it for roofing the Kaʿba. There was a Coptic carpenter in Makka, so some of what they needed to repair it was now available to them. There was also a snake that would come out of the well of the Kaʿba and bask in the sun on its wall, and they were afraid of it. God Most High sent a bird against it, which snatched it up and carried it away. Quraysh said, “We do indeed hope that God Most High has approved what we intend.” When they had resolved to demolish and rebuild it, Abū Wahb ibn ʿAmr ibn ʿĀʾidh ibn ʿAbd ibn ʿImrān ibn Makhzūm stood up and took a stone from the Kaʿba. It leapt from his hand and returned to its place. He said, “People of Quraysh, put nothing into its construction except the purest of your earnings. Let no prostitute’s fee, proceeds of a usurious transaction, or anything wrongfully taken from anyone enter into it.” These words have also been attributed to al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, but the first attribution is better established. Then Quraysh divided the Kaʿba among themselves. The side containing the door fell to Banū ʿAbd Manāf and Zuhra. The stretch between the Black Corner and the Yamānī Corner fell to Banū Makhzūm and those clans of Quraysh that joined them. The back of the Kaʿba fell to Banū Jumaḥ and Sahm, the two sons of ʿAmr ibn Huṣayṣ ibn Kaʿb ibn Luʾayy. The side adjoining the Ḥijr fell to Banū ʿAbd al-Dār ibn Quṣayy, Banū Asad ibn ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā ibn Quṣayy, and Banū ʿAdī ibn Luʾayy; this is the Ḥaṭīm, the site of the idol. Then the people shrank from demolishing it and were afraid to do so. Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra said, “I shall be the first of you to demolish it.” He took a pickaxe, then stood upon it, saying, “O God, be not alarmed, for we intend only good.” Then he demolished part of it on the side of the two corners. The people waited that night and said, “We shall see: if he is afflicted, we shall demolish nothing and shall restore it as it was. Translator's notes (6)
  • Line 1: The Arabic literally says that the mount’s “saddle” (رحلها) would not be disappointed; this is understood as referring to its rider.
  • Line 2: The two branches of Luʾayy ibn Ghālib are not identified. The syntax of the second hemistich is compressed; “among the highest” is taken as describing the praised man’s genealogical standing.
  • Line 3: جداه is read as جدّاه, “his two grandfathers.” Without vocalization it could also mean “his bounty,” but the genealogical imagery favors the former reading.
  • Line 4: This entry is prose rather than a poetic line and appears to have been included with the quotation accidentally. Its dangling opening bracket and the closing bracket supplied as h2 have been retained.
  • Line 4: أبدو كم appears to be a spacing or transcription error for أبدؤكم, “I shall begin before you / be the first of you.” The unusual invocation لم ترع is translated literally as “be not alarmed.”
  • Line 4: كالأسنة means “like spearheads,” as translated. It may be a transcription error for كالأسنمة, “like camel humps,” the reading found in the familiar account of the Kaʿba’s rebuilding.

Ibn Isḥāq said: Then the tribes of Quraysh gathered stones to build it, each tribe gathering separately. They then built it until the structure reached the position of the Black Stone. They disputed over it, each tribe wishing to lift it into its place to the exclusion of the others, until they argued, formed alliances, and prepared to fight. Banū ʿAbd al-Dār brought forward a bowl filled with blood, and they and Banū ʿAbd Allah ibn Kaʿb ibn Luʾayy pledged themselves to fight to the death. They dipped their hands in that blood and were called the Blood-Lickers. Quraysh remained in this state for four or five nights. Then they gathered in the sanctuary, consulted together, and sought an equitable settlement. It is related that Abū Umayya ibn al-Mughīra ibn ʿAbd Allah ibn ʿAmr ibn Makhzūm—who was then the oldest man in all Quraysh—said: “People of Quraysh, appoint the first person to enter through the gate of this sanctuary to judge between you in the matter over which you disagree.” They agreed, and the first to enter was the Messenger of God, may God bless him and grant him peace. When they saw him, they said: “This is the Trustworthy One. We accept him. This is Muḥammad.” When he reached them and they told him what had happened, he said, may God bless him and grant him peace: “Bring me a cloth.” It was brought to him, and he took the Black Stone and placed it upon the cloth with his own hand. Then he said: “Let each tribe take hold of one edge of the cloth, then lift it together.” They did so, and when they had raised it to its position, he set it in place with his own hand, and the building continued above it. Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā said: When the Messenger of God, may God bless him and grant him peace, placed the Stone upon the cloth, he said: “Let one man come from each of the four divisions of Quraysh.” They lifted it, and then he, may God bless him and grant him peace, set it in its place. When he had placed the Stone, he needed another stone to support it. A man from Najd went to bring him one, but he said: “No,” and instructed al-ʿAbbās—may God be pleased with him—who brought him a stone, and he propped it up. God, blessed and exalted, said: “And when We made the House a place of return for the people and a place of safety …”—the verse. By mathaba, it is meant that they are rewarded for making pilgrimage to it. It is also said to mean a gathering place, because people gather there for the hajj and the ʿumra; and it is said to mean a place of return, from their expression thabat al-ʿilla, when an illness returns. God, blessed and exalted, said: “And take the Station of Ibrāhīm as a place of prayer.” There are two accounts of why he stood upon the stone. The first is that, when the structure of the House grew high and Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, could no longer lift the stones, he stood upon this stone; thus it is the Station of Ibrāhīm. The second is that it was a stone which the wife of Ismāʿīl, peace be upon him, placed beneath Ibrāhīm’s foot when she washed his head. He placed his foot upon it, and his foot sank into it. I shall relate the circumstances in which Ibrāhīm, God’s blessings be upon him, had his head washed, abridging what the writers of prophetic history have related on the subject. When Hājar gave birth to Ismāʿīl, peace be upon him, God, glorified be He, commanded Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, to settle the two of them in Makka. He took them there, and when he wished to return, Hājar said to him, “Ibrāhīm, who commanded you to leave me in a land with neither crops nor milk-giving livestock, neither companion nor water nor provisions?” He said, “My Lord commanded me.” She said, “Then He will not abandon us.” When Ibrāhīm departed, he said, “Our Lord, You know what we conceal and what we reveal,” meaning their sorrow. When Ismāʿīl grew thirsty, he began scraping the ground with his heels. Hājar went and climbed al-Ṣafā. The valley was deep at that time. She looked out, but saw nothing, so she descended into the valley and ran through it until she emerged. Then she came to al-Marwa, climbed it, and looked out, but saw nothing. She did this seven times. Then she returned from al-Marwa to Ismāʿīl, who was scraping the ground with his heel, and the spring—Zamzam—had begun to gush. She began digging at the earth around the water with her hand. Whenever some water collected, she scooped it up with her cup and poured it into her waterskin. The Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, said, “May God have mercy on her! Had she left it alone, it would have been a flowing spring, running until the Day of Resurrection.” Jurhum were then in a valley near Makka. When the birds saw the water, they stayed in the valley. Seeing that the birds had stayed there, Jurhum said, “They would not stay there unless there were water in it.” They came to Hājar and said, “If you wish, we will stay with you and keep you company, while the water remains yours.” She said, “Yes.” They stayed with her until Ismāʿīl, peace be upon him, grew to manhood and Hājar died. He married a woman of Jurhum. Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, asked Sāra’s permission to visit Hājar. She permitted him, on condition that he not dismount. Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, arrived after Hājar had died. He went to Ismāʿīl’s house and said to his wife, “Where is your husband?” She said, “He has gone hunting.” He said, “Have you anything with which to entertain a guest?” She said, “I have neither food nor drink.” Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, said, “When your husband returns, convey my greetings to him and tell him to change the threshold of his house.” Ibrāhīm left, and Ismāʿīl returned and detected his father’s scent. He said to his wife, “Did anyone come to you?” She said, “An old man came to me, looking such-and-such,” speaking as though she thought little of him. He said, “What did he say to you?” She said, “He told me, ‘Convey my greetings to your husband and tell him to change the threshold of his door.’” So he divorced her and married another woman. Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, remained away for as long as God willed. Then he asked Sāra’s permission to visit Ismāʿīl. She permitted him, on condition that he not dismount. Ibrāhīm came to Ismāʿīl’s door, peace be upon them both, and said to his wife, “Where is your husband?” She said, “He has gone hunting and will return presently, God willing. Dismount, may God have mercy on you.” He said, “Have you anything with which to entertain a guest?” She said, “Yes.” He said, “Have you bread, wheat, barley, or dates?” She brought milk and meat, and he prayed for a blessing upon them. Had she brought bread, wheat, barley, or dates that day, Makka would have been the most abundant of God’s lands in wheat, barley, and dates. Then, seeing that his hair was disheveled, she said to him, “Dismount, may God have mercy on you, so that I may wash your head.” He did not dismount, but excused himself. She brought him the stone of the Station and placed it beneath his right side. He put his foot upon it, and the impression of his foot remained on it. Then she washed the right side of his head. She moved the stone of the Station to the left side of his head and washed it, just as she had done on the right. He said to her, “When your husband comes, convey my greetings to him and tell him, ‘The threshold of your door is now sound.’” When Ismāʿīl returned, he detected his father’s scent, peace be upon them both, and said to his wife, “Did anyone come to you?” She said, “Yes, an old man, the most handsome and fragrant of men. He said such-and-such to me, and I said such-and-such to him. I washed his head, and this is the impression of his foot. He told me, ‘Tell your husband when he comes: The threshold of your door is now sound.’” He said, “That was my father Ibrāhīm, the intimate friend of the All-Merciful, mighty and majestic.” I have related this account in abridged form only so that someone who encounters the mention of Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, having his head washed, without knowing how it happened, will not be left longing to know. He, blessed and exalted, said: “And We charged Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl: Purify My House …” There are five interpretations of this: first, of idols; second, of unbelievers; third, of impurities; fourth, of afflictions and doubts; and fifth, that it is for those who make pilgrimage to it and circumambulate it. If it is asked, “But in Ibrāhīm’s time, peace be upon him, before the House was built, was there a house to be purified?” there are two answers. The first is: “Build My House in a state of purity.” The second is: “Purify the site of My House for those who circumambulate it.” He, blessed and exalted, said: “Whoever leaves his home, emigrating to God and His Messenger, and is then overtaken by death, his reward is incumbent upon God …”—the verse. There are two interpretations. The first is that whoever leaves Makka to emigrate and dies on the road before reaching al-Madina has earned the reward for his deed and the recompense for his emigration. The second concerns someone who sets out on a military expedition and dies before the battle: he receives the reward for his striving. There is disagreement over the person concerning whom this verse was revealed. It is said that it was revealed concerning Abū Umayya Ḍamra ibn Jundab al-Khuzāʿī, who left Makka as an emigrant and died in the mountain pass. It is also said that it was revealed concerning Khālid ibn Ḥizām, the brother of Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām, who set out as an emigrant and died on the road. Ibn ʿAbbās, may God be pleased with him, said, in the account transmitted by ʿAṭāʾ: ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, may God be pleased with him, used to inform the people of Makka of whatever was revealed of the Quran. He wrote to them with the words of God, mighty and majestic: “Those whom the angels take in death while they are wronging themselves—the angels say, ‘In what condition were you?’ They say, ‘We were oppressed in the land.’ They say, ‘Was God’s earth not spacious enough for you to emigrate within it?’ Those—their abode is Hell, and evil is that destination.” When Ḥabīb ibn Ḍamra al-Laythī read this, being a very old man, he said, “Carry me, for I am not one of the oppressed, and I can certainly find the road.” They carried him on a bed toward al-Madina. When he reached al-Tanʿīm, he was close to death. He struck his right hand against his left and said, “O God, this is for You, and this is for Your Messenger. I pledge allegiance to You upon the terms on which I pledged allegiance to the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace.” Then he died. News of him reached the Companions of the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, and they said, “Had he reached al-Madina, his reward would have been more complete.” Then God, mighty and majestic, revealed: “Whoever leaves his home, emigrating to God and His Messenger, and is then overtaken by death, his reward is incumbent upon God …”—the verse. God, blessed and exalted, said: “God desires only to remove defilement from you, people of the Household, and to purify you completely.” ʿAṭāʾ ibn Abī Rabāḥ, may God have mercy on him, said: Someone who heard Umm Salama, may God be pleased with her, told me that she related: The Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, was in her house when Fāṭima, peace be upon her, brought him a pot containing khazira, a meat-and-flour porridge. She brought it in to him, and he said, “Call your husband and your two sons for me.” She said: ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan, and al-Ḥusayn, may God be pleased with them, came in to him and sat eating that porridge. He was on a bed of his upon a raised platform, with a Khaybarī cloak beneath him. She said: I was praying in the chamber when God, mighty and majestic, revealed: “God desires only to remove defilement from you, people of the Household, and to purify you completely.” The Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, took the loose portion of the cloak and covered them with it. Then he raised his hands toward heaven and said, “O God, these are the people of my household and my closest kin. Remove defilement from them, the people of the Household, and purify them completely.” She said: I put my head into the room and said, “Am I with you, Messenger of God?” He said, “You are assured of good; you are assured of good.” God, blessed and exalted, said: “O you who believe, do not enter the Prophet’s houses unless you are given permission for a meal, without waiting for it to be ready. But when you are invited, enter; and when you have eaten, disperse …”—the verse. Most commentators say: When the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, consummated his marriage to Zaynab bint Jaḥsh, may God be pleased with her, he held a wedding feast with dates and parched-grain meal, and slaughtered a sheep. Anas ibn Mālik, may God have mercy on him, said: My mother, Umm Sulaym, sent him hays, a mixture of dates, clarified butter, and dried curds, in a stone bowl. The Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, commanded me to invite his Companions, may God be pleased with them, to the meal. People kept coming, eating, and leaving. I said, “Messenger of God, I have invited people until I can find no one else to invite.” He said, “Take away your food.” They took it away, and the people departed, but three men remained talking in the house. They lingered a long time, and the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, was troubled by them, but he was exceedingly shy. Then God, mighty and majestic, revealed this verse. His words, mighty and majestic, relating the words of Ibrāhīm, God bless him and grant him peace: “My Lord, I have settled some of my offspring in an uncultivated valley beside Your Sacred House.” His offspring means Ismāʿīl, together with his mother Hājar. “In an uncultivated valley” means Makka. He settled her in its gravelly valley bed, where no one lived, trusting in God Most High and relying upon Him. “Beside Your Sacred House”: He ascribed the House to Himself, glorified be He, because no one else owns it. It is called sacred because things are forbidden there which are lawful elsewhere, such as intercourse and treating it without reverence. His words, mighty and majestic, “By the frequented House,” have four interpretations. The first is what Qatāda transmitted from Anas ibn Mālik, may God have mercy on him, who said: The Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, said, “I was taken to the seventh heaven, and the frequented House was displayed to us. It was directly opposite the Kaʿba: if it fell, it would fall upon it. Seventy thousand angels enter it every day, and when they leave it, they never return to it.” The second is that the frequented House is a house among the six heavens below the seventh, called al-Ḍurāḥ. Every day seventy thousand angels of the tribe of Iblis pray in it, never returning to it again. It stands opposite the Ancient House. The third is what al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said: The frequented House was on earth, on the site of the Kaʿba, in the time of Adam, peace be upon him, and remained until the time of Nūḥ, peace be upon him. Nūḥ commanded his people to make pilgrimage to it, but they refused and disobeyed him. When the water rose, it was lifted up and placed directly opposite its former site in the lowest heaven. Every day seventy thousand angels pass through it, and they do not return until the trumpet is blown. He said: God Most High then assigned to Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, the site of the Kaʿba, the Sacred House, as He, glorified be He, says: “And when We assigned to Ibrāhīm the site of the House …”—the verse. The fourth is what al-Ḥasan, may God be pleased with him, said: The frequented House is the Sacred House. As for “frequented,” it is said to mean that it is kept in use by people journeying to it, and it is said to mean by their remaining there. He, blessed and exalted, said: “In houses which God has permitted to be raised and His name to be remembered therein.” It is said that these are mosques, and it is said that they are houses generally. “God has permitted to be raised” means to be built, as in His words, exalted be He: “And when Ibrāhīm raised the foundations of the House,” meaning built them. It is also said to mean that requests are raised in them to God, mighty and majestic. He, blessed and exalted, said: “O you who believe, do not enter houses other than your own until you have made yourselves known and greeted their inhabitants …”—the verse. It is related from ʿAdī ibn Thābit that he said: A woman of the Ansar came and said, “Messenger of God, I am sometimes in my house in a condition in which I would not like anyone to see me, neither parent nor child. My father comes and enters, and men of my family keep coming in while I am in that condition. What should I do?” Then God Most High revealed this verse. Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq, may God be pleased with him, said, “Messenger of God, what of the inns and lodgings on the roads of al-Shām which have no inhabitants?” Then God Most High revealed: “There is no blame upon you for entering uninhabited houses …”—the verse. There are three interpretations of His words, exalted be He: “O you who believe, do not enter houses other than your own until you have made yourselves known and greeted their inhabitants.” It is said to mean: until you have asked permission. It is said to mean: until you have made your presence known to the inhabitants by clearing your throat, so that they know you are coming in. And it is said that “until you have made yourselves known” means: until you ascertain whether there is someone inside whose permission you may ask, and then greet him. Similar is His saying, exalted be He, “If you perceive sound judgment in them,” meaning if you know it to be present. Permission may be given in words or by a gesture. From Abū Hurayra, may God have mercy on him, who said: The Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, said, “A man’s messenger is his permission.” If someone asks permission three times and is not admitted, he should turn away and not renew his request. It is related from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, from al-Ashʿarī, that he asked permission to enter the presence of ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may God be pleased with him, three times. He was not admitted, so he went back. ʿUmar sent for him and said, “What made you turn back?” He said, “The Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, said, ‘Whoever asks permission three times and is not admitted should turn back.’” ʿUmar, may God be pleased with him, said, “You must bring me evidence, or I shall make an example of you.” He went to Ṭalḥa, may God have mercy on him, who testified for him. Al-Ḥasan, may God have mercy on him, said: The first request is a request for admission; the second allows consultation; the third is decisive: if they wish, they admit him, and if they wish, they turn him away. One should not ask permission while facing the doorway if the door is open. If the first of a group is admitted, permission extends to the last of them. One should not sit at the door after being turned away, for people have matters to attend to. Then He, exalted be He, said: “And greet their inhabitants.” Greeting is recommended, while asking permission is obligatory. There are two opinions concerning the greeting. The first is that it is prescribed after permission has been given, as the verse indicates by placing permission before it, and because greeting is a salutation upon meeting, and meeting takes place after permission. The second is that it is prescribed before asking permission: although it comes later in the recitation, it comes first in practice. The sense of the words is thus: “Until you greet them and ask permission.” This is based on what Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn, may God have mercy on him, related: A man asked permission to enter the presence of the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, saying, “May I enter?” The Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, said to a man who was with him, “Go and teach him how to ask permission, for he has not done it properly.” The man heard him, so he gave the greeting and asked permission. It has also been said that, if their eyes meet before permission is asked, it is preferable to greet before asking permission; but if their eyes do not meet before permission, it is preferable to ask permission before greeting. As for asking permission to enter the homes of relatives: if they are not within the degrees of kinship that prohibit marriage, one must ask their permission, just as with strangers. If they are within those degrees and the dwelling is shared, with both him and them living there, he must warn them before entering, either through his footsteps or by clearing his throat in a way that makes his presence known. The exception is one’s wife: this is not required with her, because there is no restriction upon their seeing one another’s nakedness. If the dwelling is not shared, there are two opinions about asking their permission. The first is that it is done by clearing the throat and making a sound of movement. The second is that it is done verbally, as with strangers. ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār, may God have mercy on him, related that a man said to the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, “Should I ask permission to enter my mother’s presence?” He said, “Yes.” The man said, “But I attend to her needs.” He said, “Ask her permission.” The man repeated his question three times, and he, God bless him and grant him peace, said, “Would you like to see her naked?” He said, “No.” He said, “Then ask her permission.” “If you find no one therein”—meaning anyone to give you permission—“then do not enter them until permission is given to you.” It is not permissible to peer into a dwelling to see who is inside and ask his permission, if the door is closed, because the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, said, “Asking permission was instituted only on account of sight.” If the door is open, however, someone outside it may look in, because by opening it the owner has allowed people to look. “If you are told, ‘Go back,’ then go back; that is purer for you.” If this occurs after someone has entered with permission, he must depart, and remaining is forbidden. If it occurs before entry, it is a refusal of permission and a prohibition against entering; he need only leave the place where he is standing. He, blessed and exalted, said: “There is no blame upon you for entering uninhabited houses.” These are said to be shared inns containing occupied rooms; or merchants’ shops; or lodgings for travelers and places where loads are set down, used by wayfarers on their journeys; or deserted ruins; or the houses of Makka. “In which there is some benefit for you”: it is not necessary to ask permission to enter these places. He, blessed and exalted, said: “Just as your Lord brought you out of your house in truth.” It is said to mean: Just as your Lord brought you out of Makka to al-Madina in truth, despite the reluctance of a group of the believers, so He will fulfill His promise to you by granting you victory over your enemies. It is also said that there are two interpretations of His words, exalted be He, “in truth.” The first is that you went out with the truth accompanying you. The second is that He brought you out in accordance with the obligation incumbent upon you. He, blessed and exalted, said, relating the words of the polytheists: “And they said, ‘We will not believe you until you make a spring gush forth for us from the earth; or you have a garden of palms and vines and make rivers gush abundantly through it; or you cause the sky to fall upon us in pieces, as you have claimed; or you bring God and the angels before us; or you have a house of ornament.’” [Zukhruf]: it is said to mean decoration, and it is said to mean gold. Mujāhid said, “I did not know what zukhruf meant until we heard, in the reading of Ibn Masʿūd, may God be pleased with him, ‘a house of zukhruf.’” Its root is zakhrafa, which means making an appearance beautiful. From this come the words of God, mighty and majestic: “Until, when the earth has taken on its adornment and become beautiful.” Those who addressed these words to the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, were a group of Quraysh. Ibn ʿAbbās, may God be pleased with him, said: They were ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, Shayba ibn Rabīʿa, Abū Sufyān, al-Aswad ibn al-Muṭṭalib ibn Asad, Zamʿa ibn al-Aswad, al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, Abū Jahl ibn Hishām, ʿAbd Allah ibn Abī Umayya, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, Umayya ibn Khalaf, and Nabīh and Munabbih, the two sons of al-Ḥajjāj. He, blessed and exalted, said: “Even if you had remained in your houses, those for whom killing had been decreed would have gone forth to their resting places.” There are two interpretations. The first is: Had you stayed behind, those for whom killing had been decreed would have gone forth, and their remaining at home would not have saved them. The second is: Had you stayed behind, the believers among you would have gone out and would not have stayed behind because you did. In that case the meaning of the words of God, mighty and majestic, “those for whom killing had been decreed” is those upon whom fighting had been imposed. He expressed it as killing because fighting leads to it, whether through victory or martyrdom. God, blessed and exalted, said: “A group of them ask the Prophet’s permission, saying, ‘Our houses are exposed.’” al-Suddī said: Those among them who asked his permission, God bless him and grant him peace, were two men of the Ansar: Abū ʿArāba ibn Aws and Aws ibn Qayẓī. Eighty men went back without permission. “Saying, ‘Our houses are exposed’” means: far from al-Madina, so that we fear that the unprotected women and children may be taken captive. It is also said to mean: empty, with no one in them except the vulnerable women. It is also said to mean: their walls are exposed, so that theft and attack are feared. The Arabs say aʿwara manziluka when a dwelling has lost its screening or its wall has fallen. Anything whose exposure they dislike is called ʿawra. “Had it been entered against them from its outskirts”—meaning had the hypocrites been attacked from the outskirts and surrounding districts of al-Madina—“and they had then been asked to cause discord, they would have done so.” It is said to mean: Had they been asked to fight out of tribal partisanship, they would have hastened to it. It is also said: Had they been asked to embrace polytheism, they would have responded swiftly. “And they would have delayed”—in responding to the call to discord—“only a little.” It is also said to mean that they would have remained in al-Madina only a short time before being punished. God, blessed and exalted, said: “They destroy their houses with their own hands and the hands of the believers.” It is said: with their own hands through breaking the truce, and the hands of the Muslims through fighting. It is said: with their own hands by abandoning them, and the hands of the believers by expelling them from them. It is said: with their own hands by destroying their interiors and what was inside them, so that the Muslims would not take it, and the hands of the believers by destroying their exteriors in order to reach them. It is said that their dwellings were ornamented, and they begrudged the Muslims living in them, so they destroyed them from within while the Muslims destroyed them from without. It is said that, whenever the Muslims demolished part of their fortifications, they pulled down portions of their houses to rebuild what had been destroyed of their fortifications. It is also said that their destruction of their houses consisted in this: when a settlement was reached allowing them to carry away whatever their camels could bear, they began dismantling whatever pleased them in their houses, even the pegs, to load it onto their camels. God, blessed and exalted, said: “God has made for you in your houses a place of rest.” Mujāhid said: a place in which you dwell. “And He has made for you, from the hides of livestock, houses”—these are tents—“which you find light on the day of your departure and the day of your encampment.” Thus God reminded them of His favor in providing them with the fixed houses of their homelands and the movable houses of their travels, so that His favor encompasses them both at home and on their journeys. God, blessed and exalted, said, relating the words of Nūḥ, peace be upon him: “My Lord, forgive me, my parents, and whoever enters my house as a believer.” It is said that he meant his father Lamka and his mother Mankhīl, both of whom were believers. It is also said that he meant his father and his grandfather. “And whoever enters my house as a believer”: it is said to mean my friend who enters my dwelling; or whoever enters my mosque; or whoever enters my religion. God, blessed and exalted, said: “There is no blame upon the blind, nor blame upon the lame, nor blame upon the sick, nor upon yourselves, if you eat in your own houses, or your fathers’ houses, or your mothers’ houses, or your brothers’ houses, or your sisters’ houses, or the houses of your paternal uncles, or the houses of your paternal aunts, or the houses of your maternal uncles, or the houses of your maternal aunts.” He, glorified be He, permitted eating in the houses of these people because of the bond of kinship, without asking their permission to eat, provided the food is freely available. If it is secured against them, however, they may not break into its place of safekeeping. Nor may they go beyond eating to storing food away, or to taking anything that is not food, even if it is not secured against them, except with their permission. God, blessed and exalted, said: “And We revealed to Mūsā and his brother: Establish houses for your people in Miṣr”—that is, dwellings for them to live in. As for His word “Miṣr,” it is said that it means al-Iskandariyya, and it is said that it means Miṣr itself. It is said to mean: take palaces; and it is said: mosques. “And make your houses a direction of prayer”: it is said to mean facing one another; and it is said: make your mosques face the Kaʿba. God, blessed and exalted, said: “And your Lord revealed to the bees: Take houses in the mountains, and in the trees, and in what they build.” There are three interpretations of “revealed.” The first is: He inspired them. The second is: He directed them. The third is that He, glorified be He, placed this in their instincts, something the like of which is hidden from other creatures. “Take houses in the mountains”: God Most High mentioned their houses because of the exactness of division and beauty of construction which He inspired in them and placed in their instincts. God, blessed and exalted, said: “The likeness of those who take protectors other than God is that of the spider, which takes a house for itself; and surely the frailest of houses is the spider’s house, if only they knew.” He, glorified be He, means the gods they worshiped, idols and images. “Like the spider, which takes a house for itself” means that they worshiped what could avail them nothing, like the spider’s house, which neither screens one from sight nor keeps hands away. From ʿAṭāʾ, from Ibn ʿAbbās, may God be pleased with him: He, glorified be He, mentioned the gods of the polytheists and said, “If a fly takes anything away from them, they cannot recover it from it.” He mentioned the power of the gods to contrive and likened it to the spider’s house. When God mentioned flies and spiders among what was revealed to Muḥammad, God bless him and grant him peace, they said, “What is He doing with this?” Then God, mighty and majestic, revealed: “God is not ashamed to set forth a parable, whether of a gnat or anything beyond it …”—the verse. The judge al-Māwardī, may God have mercy on him, said: [Baʿūḍa]: one of the smallest kinds of biting insects. It is so called because, in its smallness, it is like only a part, baʿḍ, of such an insect. It is said that the spider spun its web twice: once over Dāwūd, peace be upon him, and once over the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace. The plural of ʿankabūt is ʿanākib, and its diminutive is ʿunaykib. ʿAlī, may God be pleased with him, mentioned the spider in a speech of his. It is related that a man came to ʿAlī, may God be pleased with him, and said, “Commander of the Faithful, a judgment has been passed against me through which I have lost my property and my family.” He went out to al-Raḥba, and the people gathered around him. He, may God be pleased with him, said: “My honor is pledged for what I say, and I stand surety for it. Whoever is shown clearly by the lessons of the past the exemplary punishments before him will be restrained by piety from plunging into doubtful matters. The most wretched of people is a man who has scraped together learning among the rabble, without knowledge or proof. He amassed much of that of which a little would be better, and went on amassing it, until, when he had drunk his fill of stagnant, fetid water of no benefit, he sat among the people issuing legal opinions, to unravel what had confused others. Yet he is caught among fragments of uncertainties, as though in a spider’s web, not knowing whether he has been right or wrong: stumbling through darkness, riding headlong into ignorance. He has not bitten firmly into knowledge with a cutting tooth, and so gained; nor has he kept silent, and so remained safe. Blood cries out because of him, inheritances weep because of him, and through his judgment unlawful sexual relations are made lawful. Those are the ones over whom lamentation has become due while they are still alive.” What prompted the inclusion of this report was its mention of the spider. (أذن الله أن ترفع) أي تبنى، كقوله تعالى: (وإذ يرفع إبراهيم القواعد من البيت) أي يبني، وقيل: ترفع فيها الحوائج إلى الله عز وجل. وقال تبارك وتعالى: (يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تدخلوا بيوتا غير بيوتكم حتى تستأنسوا، وتسلموا على أهلها ... ) الآية. روى عن عدي بن ثابت قال: جاءت امرأة من الأنصار، فقالت: يا رسول الله إني أكون في بيتي على حال لا أحب أن يراني عليها أحد، لا والد ولا ولد، فيأتي الأب، فيدخل علي، وإنه لا يزال يدخل رجل من أهلي، وأنا على تلك الحال فكيف أصنع؟ فأنزل الله تعالى هذه الآية، فقال أبو بكر الصديق - رضوان الله عليه -: يا رسول الله أفرأيت الخانات والمساكن في طرق الشام، ليس فيها ساكن؟ فأنزل الله تعالى: (ليس عليكم جناح أن تدخلوا بيوتا غير مسكونة ... ) الآية. وفي قوله تعالى: (يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تدخلوا بيوتا غير بيوتكم حتى تستأنسوا، وتسلموا على أهلها) ثلاثة أوجه: قيل: حتى تستأذنوا. وقيل: حتى تؤنسوا أهل البيت بالتنحنح، فيعلموا بالدخول عليهم. وقيل: (حتى تستأنسوا) أي تعلموا هل فيها أحد تستأذنوه، فتسلموا عليه، ومنه قوله تعالى: (فإن آنستم منهم رشدا) ، أي علمتم. والإذن: يكون بالقول، والإشارة، عن أبي هريرة - رحمه الله - قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "رسول الرجل إذنه". فإن استأذن ثلاثا، فلم يؤذن له ولى ولم يراجع، روى عن الحسن البصري عن الأشعري: أنه استأذن على عمر بن الخطاب - رضوان الله عليه - ثلاثا، فلم يؤذن له، فرجع، فأرسل إليه عمر، فقال: ما ردك؟ فقال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "من استأذن ثلاثا، فلم يؤذن له، فليرجع" فقال عمر - رضوان الله عليه -: "لتجيئنني ببينة، وإلا جعلتك نكالا" فأتى طلحة - رحمه الله - فشهد له. قال الحسن - رحمه الله: الأولى: إذن، والثانية: مؤامرة، والثالثة: عزمة إن شاءوا إذنوا، وإن شاؤوا ردوا، ولا يستأذن وهو مستقبل الباب إن كان الباب مفتوحا، وإذا أذن لأول القوم، فقد أذن لآخرهم، ولا يقعد على الباب بعد الرد، فإن للناس حاجات. ثم قال تعالى: (وتسلموا على أهلها) والسلام ندب، والاستئذان حتم، وفي السلام قولان: [الأول] : أنه مسنون بعد الإذن على ما تضمنته الآية من تقديم الإذن عليه، ولأن السلام من تحيات اللقاء، واللقاء يكون بعد الإذن. والثاني: أنه مسنون قبل الإذن، وأنه وإن تأخر في التلاوة فهو مقدم في الحكم، وتقدير الكلام: حتى تسلموا، وتستأذنوا؛ لما روى محمد بن سيرين - رحمه الله - أن رجلا استأذن على رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: أأدخل؟ فقال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لرجل عنده: "قم فعلمه كيف يستأذن، فإنه لم يحسن" فسمعها الرجل، فسلم واستأذن. وقد قيل: إن وقعت العين على العين قبل الاستئذان، فالأولى تقديم السلام على الاستئذان، وإن لم تقع العين على العين قبل الإذن، فالأولى تقديم الاستئذان على السلام. فأما الاستئذان على منازل الأهل: فإن كانوا غير ذوي محرم لزم الاستئذان عليهم، كالأجانب. وإن كانوا ذوي محارم، وكان المنزل مشتركا، هو فيه، وهم ساكنون، لزمه قبل دخوله إنذارهم، إما بوطء أو بنحنحة مفهمة، إلا الزوجة، فلا يلزم ذلك في حقها؛ لارتفاع العورة بينهما. وإن لم يكن المنزل مشتركا، ففي الاستئذان عليهم وجهان: أحدهما: النحنحة والحركة. والثاني: بالقول، كالأجانب. وقد روى عطاء بن يسار - رحمه الله - أن رجلا قال للنبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: أستأذن على أمي؟ قال: نعم، قال: فإني أخدمها، قال: استأذن عليها، فعاوده ثلاثا، فقال صلى الله عليه وسلم: أتحب أن تراها عريانة؟ قال: لا، قال: فاستأذن عليها. (فإن لم تجدوا فيها أحدا) يعني يأذن لكم (فلا تدخلوها حتى يؤذن لكم) . ولا يجوز أن يتطلع إلى المنزل، ليرى من فيه فيستأذنه إذا كان الباب مغلقا؛ لقول النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: "إنما جعل الاستئذان لأهل البصر" إلا أن يكون الباب مفتوحا؛ فيجوز أن ينظر إذا كان خارجا منه؛ لأن صاحبه بالفتح قد أباح النظر. (فإن قيل لكم: ارجعوا، فارجعوا، هو أزكى لكم) وهذا إن كان بعد الدخول عن إذن، لزم الانصراف وحرم اللبث، فإن كان قبل الدخول، فهو رد للإذن، ومنع من الدخول، ولا يلزمه إلا الانصراف عن موقفه. وقال تبارك وتعالى: (ليس عليكم جناح أن تدخلوا بيوتا غير مسكونة) قيل: الخانات المشتركة ذوات البيوت المسكونة، وقيل: حوانيت التجار، وقيل: منازل الأسفار، ومناخات الرحال التي يرتفقون بها مارة الطريق في أسفارهم، وقيل: الخرابات المعطلة، وقيل هي بيوت مكة (فيها متاع لكم) فلا يلزم الاستئذان في هذه المنازل. وقال تبارك وتعالى: (كما أخرجك ربك من بيتك بالحق) . قيل: كما أخرجك ربك من مكة إلى المدينة بالحق مع كراهة فريق من المؤمنين، كذلك ينجز وعدك بنصرك على أعدائك. وقيل: في قوله تعالى: (بالحق) قولان: أحدهما: أنك خرجت ومعك الحق، والثاني: أنه أخرجك بالحق الذي وجب عليك. وقال تبارك وتعالى: حكاية عن المشركين: ( [وقالوا] لن نؤمن لك حتى تفجر لنا من الأرض ينبوعا [أو تكون لك جنة من نخيل وعنب فتفجر الأنهار خلالها تفجيرا. أو تسقط السماء - كما زعمت - علينا كسفا، أو تأتي بالله والملائكة قبيلا] أو يكون لك بيت من زخرف) . قيل: الزخرف: المنقوش، وقيل: الذهب. قال مجاهد: لم أكن أدري ما الزخرف، حتى سمعنا في قراءة ابن مسعود - رضي الله عنه - "بيت من زخرف" وأصله من الزخرفة، وهو تحسين الصورة، ومنه قول الله - عز وجل -: (حتى إذا أخذت الأرض زخرفها وازينت) . والذين قالوا لرسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم هذا القول نفر من قريش، قال ابن عباس - رضي الله عنه -: وهم عتبة بن ربيعة وشيبة بن ربيعة، وأبو سفيان، والأسود بن المطلب بن أسد، وزمعة بن الأسود، والوليد بن المغيرة، وأبو جهل بن هشام، وعبد الله بن أبي أمية، والعاص بن وائل، وأمية بن خلف، ونبيه، ومنبه ابنا الحجاج. وقال تبارك وتعالى: (: لو كنتم في بيوتكم لبرز الذين كتب عليهم القتل إلى مضاجعهم) فيه قولان: (الأول) : لو تخلفتم لبرز الذين كتب عليهم القتل، ولم ينجهم قعودهم. والثاني: لو تخلفتم لخرج منكم المؤمنون، ولم يتخلفوا بتخلفكم، ويكون معنى قول الله عز وجل: (الذين كتب عليهم القتل) أي فرض عليهم القتال، فعبر عنه بالقتل، لأنه يئول إليه، إما بالظفر، أو بالشهادة. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (ويستأذن فريق منهم النبي، يقولون: إن بيوتنا عورة) . قال السدي: الذي استأذنه صلى الله عليه وسلم منهم رجلان من الأنصار: أبو عرابة بن أوس، وأوس بن قيظي، ورجع ثمانون رجلا بغير إذن. (يقولون إن بيوتنا عورة) أي قاصية من المدينة، نخاف على عورة النساء والصبيان من السبي. وقيل: أي خالية، ليس فيها إلا العورة من النساء. وقيل: مكشوفة الحيطان يخاف عليها السرق والطلب، والعرب تقول: أعور منزلك، إذا ذهب ستره، أو سطق جداره، وكل ما كره انكشافه عندهم فهو عورة. (ولو دخلت عليهم من أقطارها) أي لو دخل على المنافقين من أقطار المدينة ونواحيها، (ثم سئلوا الفتنة لآتوها) قيل: سئلوا القتال في العصبية، لأسرعوا إليه. وقيل: لو سئلوا الشرك لأجابوا إليه مسرعين، (وما تلبثوا [بها] ) عن الإجابة إلى الفتنة (إلا يسيرا) وقيل: ما لبثوا في المدينة إلا قليلا حتى يعذبوا. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (يخربون بيوتهم بأيديهم وأيدي المؤمنين) . قيل: بأيديهم، لنقض الموادعة، وأيدي المسلمين بالمقاتلة. وقيل: بأيديهم في تركها، وبأيدي المؤمنين بإجلائهم عنها. وقيل: بأيديهم في خراب دواخلها وما فيها، لئلا يأخذه المسلمون، وأيدي المؤمنين في خراب ظواهرها، ليصلوا بذلك إليهم. وقيل: كانت منازلهم مزخرفة، فحسدوا المسلمين أن يسكنوها، فخربوها من داخل، وخربها المسلمون من خارج. وقيل: إنهم كانوا كلما هدم عليهم المسلمون من حصونهم شيئا نقضوا من بيوتهم ما يعمرون به ما خرب من حصونهم. وقيل: تخريبهم لبيوتهم أنهم لما صولحوا على حمل ما أقلته إبلهم، جعلوا ينقضون ما أعجبهم من بيوتهم، حتى الأوتاد، ليحملوها على إبلهم. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (والله جعل لكم من بيوتكم سكنا) قال مجاهد: موضعا تسكنون فيه (وجعل [لكم] من جلود الأنعام بيوتا) وهي الخيام (تستخفونها يوم ظعنكم، ويوم إقامتكم) فذكر الله لهم النعمة عليهم فيما جعله لهم من بيوت الأوطان الثابتة، وبيوت الأسفار المنقلة، لتعمهم النعمة في إقامتهم وأسفارهم. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: حكاية عن نوح عليه السلام - (رب اغفر لي، ولوالدي ولمن دخل بيتي مؤمنا) قيل: أراد أباه لمكا، وأمه منخيل، وكانا مؤمنين، وقيل: أراد أباه وجده. (ولمن دخل بيتي مؤمنا) قيل: صديقي الداخل إلى منزلي، وقيل: من دخل مسجدي وقيل: من دخل في ديني. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (ليس [على الأعمى حرج ولا على الأعرج حرج، ولا على المريض حرج، ولا على أنفسكم] أن تأكلوا من بيوتكم، أو بيوت آبائكم، أو بيوت أمهاتكم، أو بيوت إخوانكم، أو بيوت أخواتكم، أو بيوت أعمامكم، أو بيوت عماتكم، أو بيوت أخوالكم، أو بيوت خالاتكم) . أباح سبحانه الأكل في بيوت هؤلاء لمكان النسب من غير استئذانهم في الأكل إذا كان الطعام مبذولا، فإن كان محرزا دونهم لم يكن لهم هتك حرزه، ولا يجوز أن يتجاوز الأكل إلى الادخار، [أو] إلى ما ليس بمأكول، وإن كان غير محرز عنهم إلا بإذن منهم. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (وأوحينا إلى موسى وأخيه أن تبوآ لقومكما بمصر بيوتا) أي مساكن يسكنونها، وقيل: [في] قوله "مصر" إنها الإسكندرية، وقيل: بل هي مصر، وقيل: اتخذوا قصورا، وقيل: مساجد (واجعلوا بيوتكم قبلة) ، قيل: مقابل بعضها بعضا، وقيل: اجعلوا مساجدكم قبل الكعبة. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (وأوحى ربك إلى النخل أن اتخذي من الجبال بيوتا، ومن الشجر ومما يعرشون) . في (أوحى) ثلاثة أقوال: أحدها: ألهمها. والآخر: سخرها. والثالث: أنه سبحانه جعل ذلك في غرائزها مما يخفى مثله على غيرها. (أن اتخذي من الجبال بيوتا) ذكر الله تعالى بيوتها لما ألهمها وأودعه غرائزها من صحة القسمة، وحسن الصنعة. وقال الله تبارك وتعالى: (مثل الذين اتخذوا من دون الله أولياء، كمثل العنكبوت اتخذت بيتا، وإن أوهن البيوت لبيت العنكبوت لو كانوا يعلمون) . يعني سبحانه آلهة من أصنام وأوثان عبدوها، (كمثل العنكبوت اتخذت بيتا) يعني أنهم عبدوا ما لا يغني عنهم شيئا، كبيت العنكبوت لا يستر الأبصار، ولا يدفع الأيدي. وعن عطاء عن ابن عباس - رضي الله عنه - ذكر آلهة المشركين فقال سبحانه: (وإن يسلبهم الذباب شيئا لا يستنقذوه منه) وذكر كيد الآلهة، فجعله كبيت العنكبوت، فقالوا: - حيث ذكر الله الذباب والعنكبوت فما أنزل على محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم: أي شيء يصنع بهذا؟ فأنزل الله - عز وجل -: (إن الله لا يستحي أن يضرب مثلا ما بعوضة فما فوقها ... ) الآية. قال القاضي الماوردي - رحمه الله -: البعوضة من صغار البق، سميت بذلك لأنها كبعض البقة في صغرها. وقيل: نسجت العنكبوت مرتين: مرة على داوود عليه السلام، ومرة على النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، وجمع عنكبوت عناكب، وتصغيره عنيكب. وقد ذكر علي - It is related that a man and his wife made a pact: whichever of them died, the other would not marry again.
