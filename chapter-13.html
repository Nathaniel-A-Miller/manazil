أبو زياد الطائي، من الطويل

From ʿAbd Allah ibn Masʿūd—may God be pleased with him—who said: The Messenger of God—may God bless him and grant him peace—said: “There is no town in which someone repents but that God Most High has mercy on the people of that town and lifts punishment from them.” Ibn Masʿūd—may God be pleased with him—also said: The Messenger of God—may God bless him and grant him peace—said: “Any man who brings food to a town among the towns of the Muslims and sells it at that day’s price, seeking God’s reward, has with God the standing of a martyr.” Then he recited: “And others travel through the land, seeking of God’s bounty.” The Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib—may God’s good pleasure be upon him—said: “Five things contribute to a man’s happiness: that his wife be agreeable, his children dutiful, his brothers pious, his neighbors righteous, and his livelihood in his own town.” From al-Aṣmaʿī, who said: I heard a Bedouin say: “The worst wealth is that which is not spent; the worst brother is one who abandons you in adversity; the worst ruler is one whom the innocent fear; and the worst land is one with neither abundance nor security.” Abū Ziyād al-Ṭāʾī said:

عن عبد الله بن مسعود - رضي الله عنه - قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "ما من بلدة تاب فيها تائب إلا رحم الله تعالى أهل تلك البلدة، ورفع عنهم العذاب". وقال ابن مسعود - رضي الله عنه - قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "أيما رجل جلب طعاما إلى بلد من بلاد المسلمين، فباعه بسعر يومه محتسبا، كان عند الله بمنزلة الشهيد، ثم تلا: (وآخرون يضربون في الأرض يبتغون من فضل الله) . قال أمير المؤمنين علي بن أبي طالب - رضوان الله عليه - "من سعادة الرجل خمس: أن تكون زوجته موافقة، وأولاده أبرارا، وإخوانه أتقياء، وجيرانه صالحين، ورزقه في بلده". عن الأصمعي قال: سمعت أعرابيا يقول: شر المال ما لا ينفق، وشر الإخوان الخاذل في الشدائد، وشر السلاطين من يخافه البريء، وشر البلاد ما ليس فيه خصب ولا أمن. وقال أبو زياد الطائي:

Is it true, servants of God, that I shall not forgetmy homeland or my people, nor dwell in Najd?
أحقا عباد الله أن لست ناسيابلادي ولا قومي ولا ساكنا نجدا؟
Nor cast a glance today toward the protected pastureto soothe my heart, nor renew my acquaintance with it?
ولا ناظرا نحو الحمى اليوم نظرةأسلى بها قلبي، ولا محدثا عهدا
A land where my amulets were hung upon me,where childhood's days were fresh and full of ease.
بلاد بها نيطت على تمائميوكان بها عصر الصبي نضرا رغدا
A land where my people live, a land I love,though I have found no escape from long absence from it.
بلاد بها قومي، وارض أحبهاوإن لم أجد من طول هجرتها بدا
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 3: The wording 'نيطت على تمائمي' appears to require 'نيطت عليّ تمائمي': 'my amulets were hung upon me.' Also, 'عصر الصبي' may be a misprint for 'عصر الصبا', 'the days of childhood'; the translation follows that sense.

And Ṣadaqa ibn Nāfiʿ al-Ghanawī said:

وقال صدقة بن نافع الغنوي:

Ah, would that I knew: shall I drive my she-camelthrough Bayḍāʾ Najd, where she used to travel?
ألا ليت شعري هل أسير ناقتيببيضاء نجد حيث كان مسيرها
A land where I wore out the mount of youth,and its days and months were gentle with us.
بلاد بها أنضيت راحلة الصبيولانت لنا أيامها وشهورها
There we were free of bewildering care and its draught,and its joy brought bliss round to us.
فقدنا بها الهم المضل وشربهودار علينا بالنعيم سرورها
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: بيضاء نجد may be a place name, Bayḍāʾ Najd, or simply mean 'the white plain of Najd'; the locality is uncertain.
  • Line 2: الصبي is read as الصِّبا, 'youth,' in the metaphor 'the mount of youth.' The printed spelling may be an error; read literally, it could mean 'the boy.'
  • Line 3: وشربه is unclear. 'And its draught' is tentative; شربه could also refer to drinking companions. The intended expression, or the integrity of the text, is uncertain.

من الكامل

It is related that a secretary to the commander of the pilgrimage said: Once, while on our way to perform the pilgrimage, we camped outside al-Kūfa. Every morning I would go into al-Kūfa on my errands, and I would encounter a crippled man crawling along. I thought he was making his way toward the pilgrims to beg. One day I happened to follow him until he reached a spot overlooking the pilgrims’ tents, and I heard him say:

حكي عن كاتب لأمير الحاج أنه قال: نزلنا مرة بظاهر الكوفة، ونحن متوجهون إلى الحج، فكنت كل يوم أغدو إلى الكوفة لحوائجي، ويقابلني رجل زمن يزحف. فكنت أظنه يقصد الحاج للسؤال، فاتفق أن تبعته يوما، حتى كان بموضع يشرف منه على أخبية الحاج، فسمعته يقول:

Do you know how I bear being keptfrom the sight of the Sacred City,
أعلمت كيف تصبريعن رؤية البلد الحرام
And the two sacred sites, and a mosqueat al-Khayf, frequented every year,
والمشعرين ومسجدبالخيف يشهد كل عام
And from clinging to the two sacred sites,and from prayer at the Maqām,
وعن التزام المشعرين وعن صلاة بالمقام
And from visiting the Prophet,the Chosen One, the best of humankind?
وعن الزيارة للنبي المصطفى خير الأنام
Like the endurance of one drivenby sickness from sweet sleep.
كتصبر المدفوع بالأسقام عن طيب المنام
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 2: The precise places intended by al-mashʿarayn, “the two sacred sites,” are uncertain.
  • Line 3: Iltizām means embracing or clinging to; its application here to “the two sacred sites” is unusual, and the intended ritual action is unclear.

قال: ثم ظعنا عن الكوفة ليالي وأياما، ثم إني سمرت عند الأمير ليلة، فحدثته الحديث، فأحضر نجابا، وثلاثة نجب، وقال لي: اذهب مع هذا النجاب فأتيني به، فقلت: إن ذلك يشق علي، وأنا أدل الرسول عليه، فقال: قد علمت أنك تدل الرسول، ولكني أردت عقوبتك؛ لتأخيرك إخباري عنه، فمضيت فأتيت به. وقال أشجع السلمي:

ومغترب ينقضي ليلهفنونا ومقلته تدمع

Abū ʿAbd Allah Muḥammad ibn ʿUthmān, known as Ibn al-Ḥaddād al-Andalusī, said, elegizing a friend of his:

وقال أبو عبد الله محمد بن عثمان المعروف بابن الحداد الأندلسي يرثي صديقا له:

He knew for certain that God was the most generous of neighbors,so he resolved to depart from the abode of mortality.
تيقن أن الله أكرم جيرةفأزمع عن دار الفناء رحيلا
Though eyes are bereft of him,he has taken hearts in their stead.
فإن أقفرت منه العيون فإنهتعوض منها بالقلوب بديلا
Never have I seen such companionship turn, after him, to desolation,or such coolness upon the heart turn to burning thirst.
ولم أر أنسا بعده صار وحشةوبردا على الأكباد صار غليلا
For one whose presence made the days of joy seem short,the night of grief for him was long.
ومن كن أيام السرور قصيرةبه كان ليل الحزن فيه طويلا
Translator's notes (1)
  • Line 3: The negative construction ولم أر is awkward in context. I have understood it as an expression of astonishment, supplying an implied comparison ('such'); read strictly literally, it would deny having seen companionship turn to desolation.

An Arab woman whose paternal uncle had married her to a man from al-Shām, who then took her to al-Shām, said when she longed for her homeland:

وقالت امرأة من العرب زوجها عمها رجلا شاميا، فنقلها إلى الشام، فاشتاقت بلادها:

O my two friends, whom I regardas my trusted confidants above all who show me care,
ألا يا خليلي اللذين أراهماذوي ثقتي من دون من كان حافيا
May God water—for watering is His to grant—our lands at Ḥazm Qanāwayn with gentle morning rains:
سقى اللهوالسقيا إليه بلادنا بحزم قناوين الذهاب الغواديا
The lands of my people—by the Almighty, I love them—though I have become certain we shall not meet.
بلاد جميع، والعظيم أحبهموإن كنت قد أيقنت ألا تلاقيا
Would that I had another uncle in place of mine, and would that I had,in place of his sons, allies from Maʿadd:
ألا ليت لي عما بعمى، وليت ليمكان بنيه من معد مواليا
People who, if they feared injustice against meor oppression, would surround me with spears, standing at my back.
أناسا إذا خافوا على ظلامةوضيما أحاطوا بالقنا من ورائيا
May the Merciful never bless the face of any freebornYemeni woman who, after me, loves a Syrian.
فلا بارك الرحمن في وجه حرةيمانية بعدي تحب شآميا
Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: حافيا is uncertain here. I have tentatively understood it as someone showing care or solicitude; its more familiar meaning, “barefoot,” does not readily fit the context.
  • Line 2: Ḥazm Qanāwayn is an unidentified place; the vocalization of قناوين is uncertain. The supplied hemistich division is unusual, but has been preserved.
  • Line 3: بلاد جميع is ambiguous. I have taken جميع as a company of people, understood here as her own people, but it might instead be a proper name, Jamīʿ. والعظيم is read as an oath, “by the Almighty.”

وقال موسى بن جابر الحنفي:

And Hilāl ibn al-Asʿar al-Māzinī said:

وقال هلال بن الأسعر المازني:

I speak, having passed Naqamā, while my she-camelyearns at dawn for the two banks of Fulayj.
أقول وقد جاوزت نقمي وناقتيتحن إلى جنبي فليج مع الفجر

سقى الله يا ناق البلاد التي بهاهواك وإن عنا نأت سبل القطر
It was not through any aversion to them that separation sweptus away from their pastures and pale dunes.
فما عن قلى منا لها خفت النوىبنا عن مراعيها وكثبانها العفر
But the turns of time parted usfrom those nearest to us; a man is time’s target.
ولكن صرف الدهر فرق بينناوبين الأداني والفتى غرض الدهر
May rain water Ṣaḥrāʾ al-Ihāla, a spring encampment,and al-Waqbā—what a soft-soiled, fertile dwelling!
فسقيا لصحراء الإهالة مربعاوللوقبي من منزل دمث مثرى

Translator's notes (3)
  • Line 1: The vocalization of نقمي, rendered Naqamā, is uncertain. I take جنبي فليج as the two banks or sides of the place Fulayj; its precise geographical reference is unclear.
  • Line 2: I read القطر as quṭr, ‘region,’ and understand سبل القطر as the roads to that region. The unvocalized word can also mean ‘rain’ (Qaṭr), but the phrase would then be unclear.
  • Line 5: I could not securely identify Ṣaḥrāʾ al-Ihāla or al-Waqbā. The latter is printed الوقبي; its rendering assumes الوقبى.

And I recite verses spoken by a yearning lover,grief-stricken, saddened by dwelling sites and their traces:
وأنشدت شعرا قاله ذو صبابةكئيب شجته أربع ورسوم
May the land where Sulaymā now dwells be wateredwith rain from the clouds to quench its thirst and provide pasture.
سقى بلدا أمست سليمى تحلهمن المزن ما يروى به ويسيم
Though I am not among its inhabitants,a person dear to me dwells there.
وإن لم أكن من ساكنيه فإنهيحل به شخص على كريم

  • Line 5: Read على as عليّ ('to me') in شخص عليّ كريم, 'a person dear to me'; the supplied spelling appears to be a typing or OCR error.

قيس بن ذريح، من الطويل

And Qays ibn Dhurayḥ said:

وقال قيس بن ذريح:

There is no lover secure in his beloved,nor anyone in love, whom time will not bereave.
وما من حبيب آمن لحبيبهولا ذي هوى إلا له الدهر فاجع
It is as though God's lands, when you are not in them,are barren wastes, though full of people.
كأن بلاد الله ما لم تكن بهاوإن كان فيها الخلق قفر بلاقع
Not everything your soul promises you in solitudewill you find, nor will you follow every desire.
وما كل ما منتك نفسك خالياتلاقي، ولا كل الهوى أنت تابع
Were it not for the heart's hope that separation might relent,my ribs could not have borne it between them.
ولولا رجاء القلب أن تسعف النوىلما حملته بينهن الأضالع
I pass my day in conversation and wishes,and night brings me together with sorrow—night is a gatherer.
أقضي نهاري بالحديث وبالمنىويجمعني والهم والليل جامع
Translator's notes (2)
  • Line 1: آمن لحبيبه may mean trusting his beloved or feeling secure concerning the beloved; the translation follows the latter reading.
  • Line 5: The syntax of ويجمعني والهم والليل جامع is compressed. I understand night as the agent that brings the speaker and sorrow together.

The judge Abū al-Faraj Salama ibn Baḥr said (these verses are also attributed to the judge al-Nuʿmān al-Miṣrī):

The lament of doves warbling in Yathribstirred my longing and deepened my grief.
